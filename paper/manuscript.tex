\begin{abstract}
The 2D incompressible lid-driven cavity problem serves as a canonical model in fluid dynamics. While classical benchmarks ($Re \le 10{,}000$) focus on centerline data, open full-field archives spanning moderate to extreme Reynolds numbers ($Re = 100$ to $100{,}000$) remain scarce. We present a finite-difference framework solving the streamfunction--vorticity equations via an $\mathcal{O}(N^2 \log N)$ Discrete Sine Transform (DST-I) Poisson solver and vectorized Alternating Direction Implicit (ADI) pseudo-time continuation. For $Re \le 50{,}000$, spatial discretization retains pure second-order central differencing ($\nu_{\mathrm{num}} = 0$). At $Re = 100{,}000$, an interior hybrid scheme regularizes corner singularity dispersion with directional added viscosity $\nu_{\mathrm{art},x} = \frac{1}{2}|u|h \mathbf{1}_{Pe_x > 2}$ and $\nu_{\mathrm{art},y} = \frac{1}{2}|v|h \mathbf{1}_{Pe_y > 2}$, yielding average ratio $\bar{\nu}_{\mathrm{art}}/\nu = 1.485$ at the core, an active interior peak of $48.93$ at $y = 1-h$, and domain mean $16.32$ on $513 \times 513$ (reducing to $0.74$, $24.46$, and $8.12$ on $1025 \times 1025$). On uniform grids up to $1025 \times 1025$, stationary continuation resolves primary and secondary eddy topologies, showing qualitative alignment with Batchelor's core homogenization hypothesis ($\omega_0 \approx -2.54, x_c \to 0.50000$). Unsteady marching on $513 \times 513$ grids over finite convective windows ($t = 16.0$--$22.0$ c.t.u., $St \equiv f L / U$) resolves periodic shedding at $Re = 10{,}000$ ($St = 0.6249 \pm 0.0416$), an amplitude-modulated response at $Re = 15{,}000$ ($St = 0.5364 \pm 0.0278$), and characterized dynamics at $Re = 25{,}000$, $50{,}000$, and $100{,}000$, where $\pm \Delta f / 2$ denotes Fourier bin resolution width. All datasets are openly archived.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The incompressible flow inside a two-dimensional square cavity driven by a translating lid has been the canonical proving ground for numerical fluid algorithms for more than four decades~\cite{ghia1982high,erturk2005numerical,bruneau20062d,hou1995lattice}. Despite the simplicity of its rectangular geometry and boundary closures, the enclosed fluid exhibits rich, nonlinear physical phenomena: a dominant central gyre, secondary counter-rotating corner eddies, viscous wall boundary layers, singular velocity discontinuities at top corners, and hydrodynamic instability leading to vortex shedding and complex transitions within the two-dimensional model~\cite{peng2003transition}.

In computational fluid dynamics and high-order algorithm development, the lid-driven cavity problem is widely recognized as a standard for verifying spatial discretization schemes, benchmarking Poisson solvers, and investigating nonlinear hydrodynamic stability. However, computational engineering researchers facing this problem frequently confront severe data limitations:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Sparsity of Benchmark Data:} The canonical benchmark of Ghia, Ghia \& Shin (1982)~\cite{ghia1982high} provides velocity data along only two 1D orthogonal lines (centerlines) sampled at 17 discrete points for $Re \le 10,000$. Full 2D coordinate fields for velocity, vorticity, and pressure are rarely tabulated or accessible in modern tensor formats.
    \item \textbf{Artificial Numerical Dissipation:} At high Reynolds numbers ($Re \ge 3,200$), cell P\'eclet numbers ($Pe_x = |u| h / \nu, Pe_y = |v| h / \nu$) substantially exceed the classical monotonicity threshold of $Pe = 2$ for standard 1D linear central differencing. To prevent non-physical spatial oscillations and divergence, commercial packages and naive implementations routinely inject first-order upwind differencing or flux limiters, introducing substantial artificial numerical dissipation ($\nu_{\text{art}} \sim \mathcal{O}(h)$), artificially damping corner eddies and distorting core vortex dynamics.
    \item \textbf{Setup Friction and Closed Access:} Existing high-$Re$ benchmarks (e.g., Erturk et al., 2005~\cite{erturk2005numerical}; Erturk, 2009~\cite{erturk2009discussions}) were implemented in proprietary or legacy Fortran codes without standardized open-source distribution. Consequently, reproducing solutions requires significant manual re-implementation.
\end{enumerate}

To bridge this gap, this work develops and documents an open-source finite-difference solver built entirely in modern scientific Python (NumPy, SciPy). Operating with \emph{pure second-order central differencing} ($\nu_{\text{num}} = 0$, meaning zero added artificial dissipation $\nu_{\text{art}} = 0$) across moderate-to-high Reynolds regimes and an interior hybrid upwind scheme for the regularized model at $Re = 100{,}000$, the solver leverages a spectral Poisson solver based on the Type-I Discrete Sine Transform (DST-I), a vectorized Alternating Direction Implicit (ADI) pseudo-time marching scheme, and parameter continuation across Reynolds numbers. We systematically evaluate stationary continuation solutions from $Re = 100$ to $Re = 100{,}000$ on uniform meshes up to $1025 \times 1025$ ($1{,}050{,}625$ nodes), provide a grid convergence verification, and perform time-resolved investigations of vortex shedding over finite convective windows on $513 \times 513$ grids.

All computational flow fields, post-processing tools, and verification datasets are archived permanently under an open-source MIT license on GitHub\footnote{\url{https://github.com/KartikeyaGangwar/lid-driven-cavity-cfd}} and Zenodo~\cite{singh2026solver}\footnote{\url{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.18312938}}.

\section{Governing Equations \& Mathematical Formulation}

\subsection{Streamfunction--Vorticity System}
For 2D incompressible Newtonian fluid flow in a domain $\Omega = [0, L] \times [0, L]$, the Navier--Stokes equations in primitive variables $(\mathbf{u}, p)$ are:
\begin{align}
    \nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} &= 0, \\
    \frac{\partial \mathbf{u}}{\partial t} + (\mathbf{u} \cdot \nabla)\mathbf{u} &= -\frac{1}{\rho}\nabla p + \nu \nabla^2 \mathbf{u}.
\end{align}
Defining the non-dimensionalization based on cavity width $L$ and lid velocity $U$:
\begin{equation}
    x^* = \frac{x}{L}, \quad y^* = \frac{y}{L}, \quad \mathbf{u}^* = \frac{\mathbf{u}}{U}, \quad t^* = \frac{t U}{L}, \quad Re = \frac{U L}{\nu}.
\end{equation}
Here $t^*$ measures non-dimensional physical time in convective time units (c.t.u.). Correspondingly, oscillatory frequencies $f$ are non-dimensionalized as the Strouhal number $St \equiv f L / U$. Taking the curl of the momentum equation, pressure is eliminated. Introducing the streamfunction $\psi$ and scalar vorticity $\omega$:
\begin{equation}
    u = \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial y}, \quad v = -\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial x}, \quad \omega = \frac{\partial v}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial u}{\partial y},
\end{equation}
incompressibility is satisfied identically ($\nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} = \psi_{yx} - \psi_{xy} = 0$). The non-dimensional governing system becomes (omitting asterisks for clarity):
\begin{align}
    \frac{\partial \omega}{\partial t} + u \frac{\partial \omega}{\partial x} + v \frac{\partial \omega}{\partial y} &= \frac{1}{Re} \left( \frac{\partial^2 \omega}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2 \omega}{\partial y^2} \right), \label{eq:vorticity_transport} \\
    \nabla^2 \psi &= -\omega. \label{eq:poisson_psi}
\end{align}

\subsection{Boundary Closures \& Wall Vorticity}
\label{sec:boundary_conditions}
The physical boundary conditions for the unit square cavity are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Bottom wall ($y = 0$):} $u = 0, \quad v = 0 \implies \psi = 0$
    \item \textbf{Left wall ($x = 0$):} $u = 0, \quad v = 0 \implies \psi = 0$
    \item \textbf{Right wall ($x = 1$):} $u = 0, \quad v = 0 \implies \psi = 0$
    \item \textbf{Top moving lid ($y = 1$):} $u = U_{\text{lid}}(x), \quad v = 0 \implies \psi = 0$
\end{itemize}
For standard benchmark comparison with Ghia et al.~\cite{ghia1982high} and Erturk et al.~\cite{erturk2005numerical}, the constant lid profile $U_{\text{lid}}(x) = 1.0$ is employed.

Because the physical walls are non-porous and impermeable, the streamfunction is constant along the entire perimeter ($\psi|_{\partial \Omega} = 0$). Boundary conditions for vorticity $\omega$ are derived from the kinematic no-slip condition using a Taylor expansion into the interior domain normal to the wall (Thom's boundary closure~\cite{thom1933flow}):
\begin{align}
    \omega_{\text{bottom}}(x) &= -\frac{2 \psi(x, h)}{h^2}, \\
    \omega_{\text{left}}(y)   &= -\frac{2 \psi(h, y)}{h^2}, \\
    \omega_{\text{right}}(y)  &= -\frac{2 \psi(1-h, y)}{h^2}, \\
    \omega_{\text{top}}(x)    &= -\frac{2 \psi(x, 1-h)}{h^2} - \frac{2 U_{\text{lid}}(x)}{h}.
\end{align}
While the interior central differencing stencil is formally second-order $\mathcal{O}(h^2)$, Thom's boundary closure introduces a local truncation error of $\mathcal{O}(h)$ for boundary vorticity, which governs the global spatial accuracy of the streamfunction--vorticity system~\cite{thom1933flow,roache1998verification}. 

At the singular top lid corners $(0, 1)$ and $(1, 1)$, where the horizontal velocity abruptly jumps from the moving lid $u_{\text{lid}} = 1$ to the stationary sidewall $u = 0$, velocity is assigned to zero on the stationary boundary, and the four corner vorticity values are evaluated by taking the arithmetic average of the two adjacent boundary points:
\begin{align}
    \omega_{0, 0} &= \frac{1}{2}(\omega_{1, 0} + \omega_{0, 1}) \quad \text{(bottom-left)}, \\
    \omega_{0, N-1} &= \frac{1}{2}(\omega_{1, N-1} + \omega_{0, N-2}) \quad \text{(bottom-right)}, \\
    \omega_{N-1, 0} &= \frac{1}{2}(\omega_{N-2, 0} + \omega_{N-1, 1}) \quad \text{(top-left)}, \\
    \omega_{N-1, N-1} &= \frac{1}{2}(\omega_{N-2, N-1} + \omega_{N-1, N-2}) \quad \text{(top-right)},
\end{align}
where top-lid boundary points $\omega_{N-1, 1}$ and $\omega_{N-1, N-2}$ incorporate the lid driving term $-2 U_{\text{lid}} / h$ via Thom's condition, whereas sidewall boundary points do not.

To stabilize iterative coupling during high-$Re$ continuation and filter dispersive waves, under-relaxation is applied to the wall vorticity updates:
\begin{equation}
    \omega_{\text{wall}}^{(k+1)} = (1 - \beta_{\text{wall}}) \omega_{\text{wall}}^{(k)} + \beta_{\text{wall}} \omega_{\text{Thom}}^{(k+1)}, \quad 0 < \beta_{\text{wall}} \le 1.
\end{equation}
For physical time marching and steady continuation at moderate Reynolds numbers ($Re \le 25{,}000$), unrelaxed updates are employed ($\beta_{\text{wall}} = 1.0$), satisfying the discrete Thom condition directly without temporal filtering. At extreme Reynolds numbers ($Re = 50{,}000$ and $Re = 100{,}000$), under-relaxation ($\beta_{\text{wall}} = 0.85$ and $0.75$, respectively) acts as a discrete temporal low-pass filter on the boundary vorticity:
\begin{equation}
    \omega_{\text{wall}}^{n+1} = (1 - \beta_{\text{wall}}) \omega_{\text{wall}}^n + \beta_{\text{wall}} \omega_{\text{Thom}}^{n+1}.
\end{equation}
This filters high-wavenumber dispersive transients generated by corner velocity discontinuities on uniform Cartesian meshes. Unsteady solutions obtained under $\beta_{\text{wall}} < 1.0$ are therefore characterized as solutions of a \emph{filtered-boundary model}, and their observed dynamical frequencies are subject to this boundary regularization.

\section{Numerical Architecture \& High-Performance Algorithms}

\subsection{Fast Discrete Sine Transform (DST) Poisson Inversion}
The discrete 2D streamfunction Poisson equation (\ref{eq:poisson_psi}) on a uniform mesh of size $N \times N$ ($h = 1/(N-1)$) with homogeneous Dirichlet conditions $\psi|_{\partial \Omega} = 0$ represents an $(N-2)^2 \times (N-2)^2$ linear system. Because the 5-point discrete Laplacian on a Cartesian grid possesses analytical sinusoidal eigenmodes:
\begin{equation}
    \phi_{j, m} = \sin\left(\frac{j m \pi}{N-1}\right), \quad j, m = 1, \dots, N-2,
\end{equation}
the discrete system can be diagonalized exactly using the Type-I Discrete Sine Transform (DST-I)~\cite{swarztrauber1977methods}. Applying the 2D DST-I to both sides of $\nabla_h^2 \psi = -\omega$:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\psi}_{k_x, k_y} = \frac{\hat{\omega}_{k_x, k_y}}{\lambda_{k_x} + \lambda_{k_y}}, \quad \lambda_k = \frac{4}{h^2} \sin^2\left(\frac{k \pi}{2(N-1)}\right).
\end{equation}
Computing the 2D inverse DST-I yields $\psi_{i, j}$ exact to machine precision ($\sim 10^{-16}$) for the discrete 5-point linear Laplacian system in $\mathcal{O}(N^2 \log N)$ operations via fast algorithms (\texttt{scipy.fft.dstn}). In benchmark timings (Python 3.11, SciPy 1.11 single-threaded PocketFFT backend on Intel Core i7-12700H, timed over 100 iterations after warm-up), this spectral solve executes in approximately $0.8\,\text{ms}$ on $257 \times 257$ and $2.1\,\text{ms}$ on $513 \times 513$, eliminating iterative Poisson residual drift.

\subsection{Vectorized Alternating Direction Implicit (ADI) Marching}
The vorticity transport equation (\ref{eq:vorticity_transport}) is advanced in pseudo- or physical-time using an operator-split Alternating Direction Implicit (ADI) scheme based on the Peaceman--Rachford structure. To decouple the spatial coordinates while enabling high-performance vectorized tridiagonal solves across entire grid tensors, the convective flux $\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{u}^n, \omega^n) = u^n \delta_x \omega^n + v^n \delta_y \omega^n$ is evaluated explicitly at time level $n$, while viscous diffusion is split implicitly along alternating coordinate axes across two half-steps of size $\Delta t / 2$:
\begin{align}
    \frac{\omega^* - \omega^n}{\Delta t / 2} - \frac{1}{Re}\delta_x^2 \omega^* &= \frac{1}{Re}\delta_y^2 \omega^n - \left( u^n \delta_x \omega^n + v^n \delta_y \omega^n \right), \label{eq:adi_step1} \\
    \frac{\omega^{n+1} - \omega^*}{\Delta t / 2} - \frac{1}{Re}\delta_y^2 \omega^{n+1} &= \frac{1}{Re}\delta_x^2 \omega^* - \left( u^n \delta_x \omega^n + v^n \delta_y \omega^n \right), \label{eq:adi_step2}
\end{align}
where $\delta_x^2$ and $\delta_y^2$ denote standard second-order central difference Laplacian operators. For frozen advective fields in the interior, this directional splitting possesses a formal temporal accuracy of $\mathcal{O}(\Delta t) + \mathcal{O}(h^2)$, with unconditional linear stability for the diffusive operator. The overall global spatial accuracy is $\mathcal{O}(h)$ due to Thom's wall closure and, at $Re = 100{,}000$, the first-order upwind differencing.

Because viscous diffusion alone is inverted implicitly in each coordinate direction, the resulting tridiagonal linear systems possess spatially invariant scalar coefficients along each grid line:
\begin{equation}
    A = -\frac{\alpha_{\text{ADI}}}{2}, \quad B = 1 + \alpha_{\text{ADI}}, \quad C = -\frac{\alpha_{\text{ADI}}}{2}, \quad \text{with} \quad \alpha_{\text{ADI}} = \frac{\Delta t}{Re \cdot h^2}.
\end{equation}
This allows precomputation of the Thomas factorization arrays $(P, \text{denom})$ and admits complete vectorized sweeps across all $M = N-2$ rows (in Step 1) and columns (in Step 2) simultaneously via NumPy vectorization, bypassing per-node iterative loops.

Crucially, for all $Re \le 50,000$, all convective derivatives are discretized using \emph{pure second-order central differences}:
\begin{equation}
    (u \delta_x \omega)_{i, j} = u_{i, j} \frac{\omega_{i+1, j} - \omega_{i-1, j}}{2 h}, \quad (v \delta_y \omega)_{i, j} = v_{i, j} \frac{\omega_{i, j+1} - \omega_{i, j-1}}{2 h}.
\end{equation}
In this regime, the leading truncation error introduces zero added artificial viscosity ($\nu_{\text{num}} = 0, \nu_{\text{art}} = 0$).

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Cell P\'eclet Number Scaling and Hybrid Regularization Model ($Re = 100,000$)}{Cell Peclet Number Scaling and Hybrid Regularization Model (Re = 100000)}}
In finite-difference discretizations of convection--diffusion equations, the local cell P\'eclet number governs the spatial monotonicity of central differences. We define the signed cell P\'eclet components and their absolute magnitudes:
\begin{align}
    \widetilde{Pe}_{x, i, j} &= \frac{u_{i, j} h}{\nu} = Re \cdot u_{i, j} \cdot h, \quad Pe_{x, i, j} = |\widetilde{Pe}_{x, i, j}|, \\
    \widetilde{Pe}_{y, i, j} &= \frac{v_{i, j} h}{\nu} = Re \cdot v_{i, j} \cdot h, \quad Pe_{y, i, j} = |\widetilde{Pe}_{y, i, j}|,
\end{align}
with directional maximum $Pe_{h, i, j} = \max(Pe_{x, i, j}, Pe_{y, i, j})$.

The threshold $Pe_h \le 2$ represents the monotonicity condition for the standard 1D linear steady central convection--diffusion stencil, below which spurious node-to-node oscillations (wiggles) are absent. On a uniform $513 \times 513$ mesh ($h = 1/512 \approx 1.953 \times 10^{-3}$) with lid velocity $U = 1.0$, the peak cell P\'eclet number scales linearly with Reynolds number:
\begin{align}
    Re = 1,000  &\implies Pe \approx 1.95 < 2 \quad \text{(monotonic central stencil)}, \notag \\
    Re = 10,000 &\implies Pe \approx 19.53, \notag \\
    Re = 50,000 &\implies Pe \approx 97.66, \notag \\
    Re = 100,000 &\implies Pe \approx 195.31 \gg 2.
\end{align}
Simultaneously, the physical laminar boundary layer thickness scales asymptotically as $\delta \sim L / \sqrt{Re}$. At $Re = 100,000$, $\delta \approx 1/\sqrt{100,000} \approx 3.16 \times 10^{-3}$, providing only $\delta / h \approx 1.62$ grid intervals across the wall shear layer on the $513 \times 513$ grid.

At the discontinuous driving lid corners $(x, y) = (0, 1)$ and $(1, 1)$, where velocity abruptly jumps from $u_{\text{lid}} = 1$ to $u_{\text{wall}} = 0$, the mathematical singularity produces unbounded local vorticity gradients $\partial \omega / \partial x \to \infty$. When $Pe_h \approx 195.3 \gg 2$, pure central differencing excites high-frequency dispersive waves along the walls, eventually inducing numerical divergence on uniform Cartesian meshes.

To regularize this singularity dispersion on uniform grids, we incorporate an interior hybrid differencing formulation based on local directional cell P\'eclet numbers for $Re = 100{,}000$. Using the signed cell P\'eclet numbers, convective derivatives are evaluated piecewise:
\begin{equation}
    (u \delta_x \omega)_{i, j} = \begin{cases}
        u_{i, j} \frac{\omega_{i+1, j} - \omega_{i-1, j}}{2 h}, & |\widetilde{Pe}_{x, i, j}| \le 2, \\
        u_{i, j} \frac{\omega_{i, j} - \omega_{i-1, j}}{h}, & \widetilde{Pe}_{x, i, j} > 2, \\
        u_{i, j} \frac{\omega_{i+1, j} - \omega_{i, j}}{h}, & \widetilde{Pe}_{x, i, j} < -2,
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
with an identical directional formulation along the $y$-axis based on $\widetilde{Pe}_{y, i, j}$.

In our operator-split ADI implementation, the implicit tridiagonal operators retain full physical molecular diffusion $(1/Re)\nabla^2 \omega$ in both half-steps. Directional upwinding introduces a leading truncation diffusion tensor $\boldsymbol{\nu}_{\text{art}} = \text{diag}(\nu_{\text{art}, x}, \nu_{\text{art}, y})$, where:
\begin{equation}
    \nu_{\text{art}, x} = \frac{|u_{i, j}| h}{2} \mathbf{1}_{Pe_x > 2}, \quad \nu_{\text{art}, y} = \frac{|v_{i, j}| h}{2} \mathbf{1}_{Pe_y > 2}.
\end{equation}
The total effective diffusion in an activated coordinate direction is $\nu_{\text{eff}, x} = \nu + \nu_{\text{art}, x}$ and $\nu_{\text{eff}, y} = \nu + \nu_{\text{art}, y}$. This artificial dissipation is anisotropic: at the cavity geometric center $(0.5, 0.5)$ for $Re = 100{,}000$ on $513 \times 513$, velocities are $u \approx -0.0304, v \approx -0.0036$, giving $Pe_x \approx 5.94$ and $Pe_y \approx 0.70 < 2$. Thus, only the $x$-direction is upwinded ($\nu_{\text{art}, x} / \nu = 2.97$, while $\nu_{\text{art}, y} = 0$). Defining the directional average artificial viscosity ratio:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\bar{\nu}_{\text{art}}}{\nu} = \frac{1}{2}\left( \frac{\nu_{\text{art}, x}}{\nu} + \frac{\nu_{\text{art}, y}}{\nu} \right) = \frac{1}{4} \left[ Pe_x \mathbf{1}_{Pe_x > 2} + Pe_y \mathbf{1}_{Pe_y > 2} \right]. \label{eq:excess_visc}
\end{equation}
At the core center, $\bar{\nu}_{\text{art}} / \nu = \frac{1}{4}(5.94) = 1.485$. Across the active interior nodes (excluding boundary nodes where Thom closures replace transport), the discrete maximum artificial dissipation occurs at $y = 1-h = 0.99805$, reaching $\bar{\nu}_{\text{art}} / \nu = 48.93$ (with nominal lid boundary limit $48.83$). The domain-averaged added viscosity is $\langle \bar{\nu}_{\text{art}} / \nu \rangle = 16.32$.

Figure~\ref{fig:peclet_dissipation_map} provides a spatial audit of the hybrid differencing regime and numerical dissipation at $Re = 100,000$. Pure central differencing ($Pe \le 2, \bar{\nu}_{\text{art}} = 0$) is active across $1.79\%$ of the domain area, concentrated along lines of zero velocity and near stagnation points. Across the remaining $98.21\%$ of the interior domain, the hybrid formulation activates directional upwinding, providing interior numerical stabilization that suppresses high-wavenumber dispersive corner shock waves. 

Importantly, as depicted in Figure~\ref{fig:peclet_dissipation_map}(c), the magnitude of numerical dissipation is strongly non-uniform. In the cavity geometric core $(0.5, 0.5)$, the added viscosity ratio is $\bar{\nu}_{\text{art}} / \nu = 1.485$ ($\bar{\nu}_{\text{art}} \approx 1.49\nu$). The peak numerical viscosity ratio $\bar{\nu}_{\text{art}} / \nu = 48.83$ (discrete active interior maximum $48.93$) is confined strictly to the high-speed near-lid shear layer ($Pe_{\max} = 195.31$). 

On the $513 \times 513$ grid, directional upwinding is active across $98.21\%$ of the interior domain (pure central differencing operates on $1.79\%$, near stagnation regions). Consequently, the computation at $Re = 100{,}000$ represents a hybrid-regularized numerical model rather than an unstabilized discretization of the original Navier--Stokes equations. 

Upon grid refinement to $1025 \times 1025$ ($h = 1/1024$), cell spacing is halved, which directly reduces artificial dissipation: the central differencing area expands to $3.09\%$, the peak active interior dissipation drops to $\bar{\nu}_{\text{art}} / \nu \le 24.46$, the core center dissipation drops to $\bar{\nu}_{\text{art}} / \nu = 0.74$, and the domain-mean added viscosity drops to $8.12\nu$. 

We emphasize that for all moderate and high Reynolds numbers up to $Re = 50,000$, our solver executes with \emph{pure second-order central differencing everywhere} ($\nu_{\text{art}} = 0$ identically across $100\%$ of the domain). The hybrid formulation is invoked selectively at $Re = 100,000$ as a regularized model to enable stable computation and suppress corner singularity dispersion.

\subsection{Reynolds Number Homotopy Continuation Ladder}
At extreme Reynolds numbers ($Re \ge 5,000$), impulsive starts from quiescent initial conditions ($\mathbf{u} = 0, \psi = 0$) generate catastrophic corner boundary layer detachments that destabilize central difference operators. To overcome this startup shock, we implement a parameter homotopy continuation ladder:
\begin{align}
    \mathcal{H}(Re) : 100 &\longrightarrow 1,000 \longrightarrow 3,200 \longrightarrow 5,000 \longrightarrow 7,500 \longrightarrow 10,000 \notag \\
    &\longrightarrow 15,000 \longrightarrow 20,000 \longrightarrow 25,000 \longrightarrow 30,000 \longrightarrow 35,000 \notag \\
    &\longrightarrow 40,000 \longrightarrow 45,000 \longrightarrow 50,000 \longrightarrow 100,000.
\end{align}
The converged velocity and vorticity fields of stage $Re_m$ serve as the warm-start initial condition for stage $Re_{m+1}$. For inter-grid transitions, a smooth bicubic spline interpolation operator prolongs coarse-mesh flow fields to finer meshes with boundary re-normalization.

\section{Stationary Continuation Solutions and Benchmark Comparisons (\texorpdfstring{$Re = 100 \to 100{,}000$}{Re = 100 to 100000})}

\subsection{Primary and Secondary Eddy Benchmark Comparison}
We systematically compare the solver against the canonical benchmark data of Ghia et al. (1982)~\cite{ghia1982high} and the high Reynolds number numerical study of Erturk et al. (2005, 2009)~\cite{erturk2005numerical,erturk2009discussions}. For Reynolds numbers above the primary instability threshold ($Re > Re_{\text{cr}} \approx 8,000$), physical steady states of the 2D Navier--Stokes equations lose temporal stability to oscillatory perturbations. In physical time-accurate marching, perturbations naturally grow along unstable eigenmodes. In our continuation framework, convergence to stationary solutions is achieved using a coupled false-transient ADI marching scheme with Reynolds parameter homotopy continuation: each stage begins with the converged solution of the preceding Reynolds number as a warm-start. Under-relaxation of the wall vorticity updates ($\beta_{\text{wall}}$) and the directional operator splitting act as a numerical continuation filter that damps physical temporal oscillations, guiding the iterations toward the symmetric stationary fixed point of the discrete steady operator (the stationary continuation branch). Iterative convergence is monitored using the pseudo-time increment criterion $\|\omega^{k+1} - \omega^k\|_\infty < 10^{-5}$ across all interior nodes. \subsection{\texorpdfstring{Core Vorticity Homogenization \& Batchelor's Hypothesis ($Re \to 100,000$)}{Core Vorticity Homogenization and Batchelor's Hypothesis (Re -> 100,000)}}
A prominent physical feature of two-dimensional recirculating flows at high Reynolds numbers is the homogenization of vorticity within closed streamline regions. According to the classical asymptotic theorem of Batchelor (1956)~\cite{batchelor1956steady}, in the limit $Re \to \infty$, steady Navier--Stokes solutions with closed streamlines must approach a state of uniform core vorticity $\nabla \omega \approx 0$:
\begin{equation}
    \lim_{Re \to \infty} \nabla \omega(x, y) = 0 \quad \text{for } (x, y) \in \Omega_{\text{core}}.
\end{equation}
Batchelor's theorem establishes spatial uniformity of the core vorticity rather than prescribing a specific numerical value $\omega_0$. For an idealized square cavity with regularized boundary layers and uniform perimeter dissipation, Burggraf (1966)~\cite{burggraf1966analytical} derived an asymptotic theoretical limit of $\omega_0 \approx -1.886$ (under the present clockwise sign convention). We define the core vorticity $\omega_0$ as the spatial mean over $y \in [0.35, 0.65]$ along the vertical centerline $x = 0.5$:
\begin{equation}
    \omega_0 = \frac{1}{0.30} \int_{0.35}^{0.65} \omega(0.5, y) \, dy.
\end{equation}
In our numerical continuation solutions across $Re = 10{,}000$--$100{,}000$, the computed core vorticity settles at $\omega_0 = -2.5719$ at $Re = 10{,}000$, $-2.5453$ at $Re = 50{,}000$, and $-2.5418$ at $Re = 100{,}000$ (with standard deviation $\approx 0.10$ across the span). These profiles exhibit qualitative homogenization ($\nabla \omega \approx 0$), consistent with Batchelor's core hypothesis. However, we emphasize that Batchelor's theorem addresses steady solutions of the original Navier--Stokes equations with closed streamlines as $Re \to \infty$. Because our solutions at $Re \ge 50{,}000$ incorporate numerical boundary filtering ($\beta_{\text{wall}} < 1.0$) and, at $Re = 100{,}000$, directional hybrid upwinding, these computations represent an investigation of the regularized numerical model rather than a direct physical confirmation of the inviscid Navier--Stokes limit. The numerical offset from Burggraf's $-1.886$ limit reflects the singular top-corner velocity discontinuities, the displacement effect of large corner recirculation eddies, and the introduced numerical regularization.

Concurrently, high-Reynolds numerical continuation solutions exhibit an accompanying geometric restructuring: as viscous wall boundary layers contract and secondary eddy systems reach topological balance, the primary recirculating core center migrates horizontally from $(0.5117, 0.5215)$ at $Re = 10,000$ toward the geometric mid-plane ($x_c \to 0.50000$ at $Re = 50,000$, and $0.4990$ on $1025 \times 1025$), while the core minimum streamfunction stabilizes at $\psi_{\min} \approx -0.12572$.

\subsection{Formal Grid Convergence Index (GCI) and Richardson Extrapolation}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Triplet 1 ($Re = 10,000$):} $N_1 = 513 \times 513$ ($h_1 = 1/512$), $N_2 = 257 \times 257$ ($h_2 = 1/256$), $N_3 = 129 \times 129$ ($h_3 = 1/128$).
    \item \textbf{Triplet 2 ($Re = 100,000$):} $N_1 = 1025 \times 1025$ ($h_1 = 1/1024$), $N_2 = 513 \times 513$ ($h_2 = 1/512$), $N_3 = 257 \times 257$ ($h_3 = 1/256$).
\end{itemize}

For each triplet, the observed order of convergence $p$, Richardson extrapolated continuum solution ($h \to 0$), approximate relative errors $e_a^{21} = |(f_1 - f_2)/f_1|$ and $e_a^{32} = |(f_2 - f_3)/f_2|$, and fine-grid GCI with safety factor $F_s = 1.25$ are computed via:
\begin{equation}
    p = \frac{\ln |(f_3 - f_2) / (f_2 - f_1)|}{\ln(r)}, \quad f_{h=0} = f_1 + \frac{f_1 - f_2}{r^p - 1}, \quad \text{GCI}_{21} = \frac{F_s \cdot e_a^{21}}{r^p - 1}, \quad \text{GCI}_{32} = \frac{F_s \cdot e_a^{32} \cdot r^p}{r^p - 1}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Unsteady Vortex Shedding on \texorpdfstring{$513 \times 513$}{513x513} (\texorpdfstring{$Re = 10,000$}{Re = 10000})}
In the two-dimensional incompressible Navier--Stokes equations for the lid-driven cavity, the steady symmetric state undergoes a supercritical Hopf bifurcation as the Reynolds number is increased. While spectral collocation investigations using smooth, regularized lid velocities (e.g., Peng, Shiau \& Hwang 2003~\cite{peng2003transition}) identify the linear instability onset near $Re_{\text{cr}} \approx 7,400$ ($St \approx 0.59$), finite-difference formulations with discontinuous lid corners (e.g., Bruneau \& Saad 2006~\cite{bruneau20062d}) identify the primary instability threshold in the interval $8,000 \le Re_{\text{cr}} \le 8,050$ with shedding frequency near $St \approx 0.45$ at $Re = 8,100$.

To investigate post-bifurcation periodic dynamics in the 2D model, we performed time-accurate marching without wall relaxation ($\beta_{\text{wall}} = 1.0$) directly on the $513 \times 513$ mesh over $44,000$ time steps ($\Delta t = 5.0 \times 10^{-4}$ convective time units, reaching $t = 22.0$ with $CFL \approx 0.256$). A localized spatial perturbation $\delta \omega = 0.05 \exp(-[(x-0.8)^2 + (y-0.8)^2]/0.05^2)$ was seeded into the upper shear layer to trigger the unstable linear mode. Velocity telemetry was logged continuously every $10$ time steps ($\Delta t_{\text{sample}} = 0.005$) at the bottom-left secondary eddy detachment zone $(x=0.08, y=0.15)$.

\subsection{Diagnostic Pressure Poisson Formulation and Discrete Solver}
\label{sec:pressure_reconstruction}
To reconstruct the scalar pressure field $p(x, y)$ from the streamfunction--vorticity solution in post-processing, we formulate the pressure Poisson equation derived from the divergence of the 2D incompressible Navier--Stokes momentum equations:
\begin{equation}
    \nabla^2 p = 2 \left[ \left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}\right)\left(\frac{\partial v}{\partial y}\right) - \left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\right)\left(\frac{\partial v}{\partial x}\right) \right] = - \left[ \left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}\right)^2 + 2\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\frac{\partial v}{\partial x} + \left(\frac{\partial v}{\partial y}\right)^2 \right], \label{eq:pressure_poisson}
\end{equation}
under the assumption of incompressibility $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} = 0$. In our implementation (\path{lid_driven_cavity_fdm.py}), equation~(\ref{eq:pressure_poisson}) is discretized on the uniform Cartesian mesh ($h = \Delta x = \Delta y$) using the standard discrete 5-point Laplacian stencil for interior nodes ($1 \le i, j \le N-2$):
\begin{equation}
    \nabla_h^2 p_{i, j} \equiv \frac{p_{i+1, j} + p_{i-1, j} + p_{i, j+1} + p_{i, j-1} - 4 p_{i, j}}{h^2} = S_{i, j},
\end{equation}
where the discrete source term $S_{i, j}$ is evaluated using second-order central differences applied to the reconstructed velocity components:
\begin{equation}
    S_{i, j} = - \left[ \left(\delta_x u_{i, j}\right)^2 + 2 (\delta_y u_{i, j}) (\delta_x v_{i, j}) + \left(\delta_y v_{i, j}\right)^2 \right],
\end{equation}
with $\delta_x \phi_{i, j} = (\phi_{i+1, j} - \phi_{i-1, j})/(2h)$ and $\delta_y \phi_{i, j} = (\phi_{i, j+1} - \phi_{i, j-1})/(2h)$.

Because velocity boundary conditions are enforced on the cavity walls, momentum-consistent wall pressure boundary conditions would require evaluating normal viscous stress projections $\partial p / \partial n = \frac{1}{Re}\nabla^2 u_n$. As a simplified diagnostic surrogate, the solver applies discrete homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions via first-order ghost-node copying:
\begin{equation}
    p_{0, j} = p_{1, j}, \quad p_{N-1, j} = p_{N-2, j}, \quad p_{i, 0} = p_{i, 1}, \quad p_{i, N-1} = p_{i, N-2}.
\end{equation}
The discrete system is advanced via Jacobi relaxation:
\begin{equation}
    p_{i, j}^{(k+1)} = \frac{1}{4} \left( p_{i+1, j}^{(k)} + p_{i-1, j}^{(k)} + p_{i, j+1}^{(k)} + p_{i, j-1}^{(k)} - h^2 S_{i, j} \right),
\end{equation}
until the maximum step increment satisfies $\max_{1 \le i, j \le N-2} |p_{i, j}^{(k+1)} - p_{i, j}^{(k)}| < 10^{-5}$ (or a maximum of $2500$ iterations). To fix the arbitrary additive pressure constant resulting from the pure Neumann boundary condition, a zero-mean gauge is projected across the interior domain nodes:
\begin{equation}
    p_{i, j} \leftarrow p_{i, j} - \frac{1}{(N-2)^2} \sum_{k=1}^{N-2} \sum_{m=1}^{N-2} p_{k, m}.
\end{equation}
We emphasize that this diagnostic pressure field serves for qualitative flow topology visualization; users requiring momentum-consistent wall pressure distributions should note that the diagnostic Neumann boundary condition neglects boundary-normal viscous diffusion terms.

\section{Conclusion}
This study presented a high-resolution finite-difference framework for 2D lid-driven cavity flows, computing stationary continuation solutions up to $Re = 100,000$ on uniform Cartesian meshes up to $1025 \times 1025$ ($1,050,625$ nodes) and observing finite-window unsteady dynamics on $513 \times 513$ meshes ($263,169$ nodes). Combining a direct $\mathcal{O}(N^2 \log N)$ Discrete Sine Transform (DST-I) Poisson solver with second-order central differencing and continuation warm-starts, the solver operates with zero artificial numerical diffusion ($\nu_{\text{art}} = 0$) across moderate-to-high Reynolds numbers ($Re \le 50,000$). At $Re = 100,000$, where cell P\'eclet numbers exceed $195$, an interior hybrid upwind stabilization is introduced, which we formally classify as a hybrid-regularized numerical model with directional artificial diffusion tensor $\boldsymbol{\nu}_{\text{art}}$. 

Geometric comparison against Ghia et al. (1982) and Erturk et al. (2005, 2009) demonstrates close primary vortex center agreement ($\Delta = 0.00688$ at $Re = 25,000$), alongside a consistent $\sim 7.5\%$ streamfunction extremum shift relative to Erturk's non-uniform compact formulation. At $Re = 50,000$ and $Re = 100,000$, the primary vortex core center approaches mid-plane symmetry ($x_c = 0.50000$ on $513 \times 513$ and $x_c = 0.4990$ on $1025 \times 1025$) with $\psi_{\min} = -0.12572$, exhibiting qualitative behavior consistent with Batchelor's core homogenization hypothesis within the regularized model ($\bar{\omega}_0 \approx -2.54$). Time-resolved simulations in the 2D model capture periodic vortex shedding ($St = 0.6249 \pm 0.0416$ at $Re = 10,000$, $7.50$ cycles), an amplitude-modulated multi-frequency response at $Re = 15,000$ ($St = 0.5364 \pm 0.0278$, $9.66$ cycles), and nonlinear wall shedding across extended horizons: $Re = 25,000$ ($St = 0.0953 \pm 0.0281$, $1.70$ cycles), $Re = 50,000$ ($St = 0.2581 \pm 0.0300$, $4.30$ cycles), and $Re = 100,000$ ($St = 0.1908 \pm 0.0379$, $2.52$ cycles). For $Re \ge 50,000$, wall relaxation ($\beta_{\text{wall}} < 1.0$) acts as a temporal boundary filter, scoping the results to a filtered-boundary model.

By openly releasing all 30 verified datasets, source code, and reproduction pipelines under the MIT license, this work provides a transparent, auditable reference for numerical method development in two-dimensional cavity flows.
