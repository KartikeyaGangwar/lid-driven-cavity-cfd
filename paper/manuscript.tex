\begin{abstract}
The 2D incompressible lid-driven cavity problem serves as a canonical model in fluid dynamics. While classical benchmarks ($Re \le 10{,}000$) focus on centerline data, open full-field archives spanning moderate to extreme Reynolds numbers ($Re = 100$ to $100{,}000$) remain scarce. We present a finite-difference framework solving the streamfunction--vorticity equations via an $\mathcal{O}(N^2 \log N)$ Discrete Sine Transform (DST-I) Poisson solver and vectorized Alternating Direction Implicit (ADI) pseudo-time continuation. For $Re \le 50{,}000$, spatial discretization retains pure second-order central differencing ($\nu_{\mathrm{art}} = 0$). At $Re = 100{,}000$, an interior hybrid scheme regularizes corner singularity dispersion with directional added viscosity $\nu_{\mathrm{art},x} = \frac{1}{2}|u|h \mathbf{1}_{Pe_x > 2}$ and $\nu_{\mathrm{art},y} = \frac{1}{2}|v|h \mathbf{1}_{Pe_y > 2}$, yielding average ratio $\bar{\nu}_{\mathrm{art}}/\nu = 1.485$ at the core, an active interior peak of $48.93$ at $y = 1-h$, and domain mean $16.32$ on $513 \times 513$ (reducing to $0.74$, $24.46$, and $8.12$ on $1025 \times 1025$). On uniform grids up to $1025 \times 1025$, stationary continuation resolves primary and secondary eddy topologies, showing qualitative alignment with Batchelor's core homogenization hypothesis ($\omega_0 \approx -2.54, x_c \to 0.50000$). Unsteady marching on $513 \times 513$ grids over finite convective windows ($t = 16.0$--$22.0$ c.t.u., $St \equiv f L / U$) resolves periodic shedding at $Re = 10{,}000$ ($St = 0.6249 \pm 0.0416$), an amplitude-modulated response at $Re = 15{,}000$ ($St = 0.5364 \pm 0.0278$), and characterized dynamics at $Re = 25{,}000$, $50{,}000$, and $100{,}000$, where $\pm \Delta f / 2$ denotes Fourier bin resolution width. All datasets are openly archived.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Incompressible flow within a square cavity driven by a moving lid remains a foundational benchmark in computational fluid dynamics~\cite{ghia1982high,erturk2005numerical,bruneau20062d,hou1995lattice}. The geometry is elementary. The physics, however, are demanding. The flow develops an energetic primary gyre, recirculating corner eddy systems whose self-similar hierarchy is governed asymptotically by Moffatt's eddy theory~\cite{moffatt1964viscous}, steep wall shear layers whose laminar thickness contracts with Reynolds number~\cite{prandtl1904uber,schlichting2000boundary}, and velocity discontinuities at the upper driven corners~\cite{botella1998benchmark,bruneau20062d}. In physical three-dimensional configurations, the flow loses stability at moderate Reynolds numbers ($Re \sim 1{,}000\text{--}3{,}200$) through centrifugal instabilities producing spanwise Taylor--G\"ortler vortices~\cite{koseff1984hydrothermal,aidun1991primary,albensoeder2001three}. Under the two-dimensional mathematical idealization investigated here, however, spanwise modes are absent, and the steady solution branch persists until undergoing a 2D supercritical Hopf bifurcation to periodic vortex shedding at $Re \approx 8{,}000$~\cite{shen1991hopf,peng2003transition,bruneau20062d}.

Despite widespread adoption of the cavity for numerical verification, researchers face three practical bottlenecks:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Data Sparsity:} The standard benchmark of Ghia, Ghia \& Shin (1982)~\cite{ghia1982high} tabulates velocities along only two 1D orthogonal lines (centerlines) at 17 discrete points for $Re \le 10{,}000$. Comprehensive 2D coordinate tensors for velocity, vorticity, and pressure remain unavailable in modern open formats.
    \item \textbf{Artificial Numerical Diffusion:} At elevated Reynolds numbers ($Re \ge 3{,}200$), cell P\'eclet numbers exceed two. To suppress spurious spatial grid oscillations (commonly termed "grid wiggles"), commercial solvers and textbook implementations routinely inject first-order upwind differencing or numerical limiters~\cite{patankar1980numerical,spalding1972novel,roache1998verification,ferziger2002computational}. The resulting artificial numerical dissipation ($\nu_{\text{art}} \sim \mathcal{O}(h)$) unphysically damps secondary corner eddies and alters core vortex dynamics.
    \item \textbf{Reproducibility Hurdles:} Higher-$Re$ solutions (e.g., Erturk et al.~\cite{erturk2005numerical,erturk2009discussions}) have historically relied on closed or legacy codes, forcing investigators to reconstruct implementations from scratch.
\end{enumerate}

This study presents an open-source finite-difference framework implemented in vectorized Python (NumPy, SciPy). The framework pairs an exact Discrete Sine Transform (DST-I) Poisson solver with a vectorized Alternating Direction Implicit (ADI) marching scheme. Spatial discretization preserves pure second-order central differencing ($\nu_{\text{art}} = 0$) for all Reynolds numbers up to $Re = 50{,}000$. At $Re = 100{,}000$, where cell P\'eclet numbers exceed 195, an interior hybrid upwind scheme stabilizes the singular corner shocks under an explicitly defined regularized model. We compute stationary continuation solutions from $Re = 100$ to $100{,}000$ on uniform Cartesian meshes up to $1025 \times 1025$ ($1{,}050{,}625$ nodes), examine grid convergence via the ASME V\&V 20 standard, and track finite-window unsteady vortex shedding on $513 \times 513$ grids.

All flow fields, processing utilities, and verification datasets are permanently archived under the MIT license on GitHub\footnote{\url{https://github.com/KartikeyaGangwar/lid-driven-cavity-cfd}} and Zenodo~\cite{singh2026solver}\footnote{\url{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.18312938}}.

\section{Governing Equations \& Mathematical Formulation}

\subsection{Streamfunction--Vorticity System}
For 2D incompressible Newtonian fluid flow in a domain $\Omega = [0, L] \times [0, L]$, the Navier--Stokes equations in primitive variables $(\mathbf{u}, p)$ are:
\begin{align}
    \nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} &= 0, \\
    \frac{\partial \mathbf{u}}{\partial t} + (\mathbf{u} \cdot \nabla)\mathbf{u} &= -\frac{1}{\rho}\nabla p + \nu \nabla^2 \mathbf{u}.
\end{align}
Defining the non-dimensionalization based on cavity width $L$ and lid velocity $U$:
\begin{equation}
    x^* = \frac{x}{L}, \quad y^* = \frac{y}{L}, \quad \mathbf{u}^* = \frac{\mathbf{u}}{U}, \quad t^* = \frac{t U}{L}, \quad Re = \frac{U L}{\nu}.
\end{equation}
Here $t^*$ measures non-dimensional physical time in convective time units (c.t.u.). Correspondingly, oscillatory frequencies $f$ are non-dimensionalized as the Strouhal number $St \equiv f L / U$. Taking the curl of the momentum equation, pressure is eliminated. Introducing the streamfunction $\psi$ and scalar vorticity $\omega$:
\begin{equation}
    u = \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial y}, \quad v = -\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial x}, \quad \omega = \frac{\partial v}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial u}{\partial y},
\end{equation}
incompressibility is satisfied identically ($\nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} = \psi_{yx} - \psi_{xy} = 0$). The non-dimensional governing system becomes (omitting asterisks for clarity):
\begin{align}
    \frac{\partial \omega}{\partial t} + u \frac{\partial \omega}{\partial x} + v \frac{\partial \omega}{\partial y} &= \frac{1}{Re} \left( \frac{\partial^2 \omega}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2 \omega}{\partial y^2} \right), \label{eq:vorticity_transport} \\
    \nabla^2 \psi &= -\omega. \label{eq:poisson_psi}
\end{align}

\subsection{Boundary Closures \& Wall Vorticity}
\label{sec:boundary_conditions}
The physical boundary conditions for the unit square cavity are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Bottom wall ($y = 0$):} $u = 0, \quad v = 0 \implies \psi = 0$
    \item \textbf{Left wall ($x = 0$):} $u = 0, \quad v = 0 \implies \psi = 0$
    \item \textbf{Right wall ($x = 1$):} $u = 0, \quad v = 0 \implies \psi = 0$
    \item \textbf{Top moving lid ($y = 1$):} $u = U_{\text{lid}}(x), \quad v = 0 \implies \psi = 0$
\end{itemize}
For standard benchmark comparison with Ghia et al.~\cite{ghia1982high} and Erturk et al.~\cite{erturk2005numerical}, the constant lid profile $U_{\text{lid}}(x) = 1.0$ is employed.

Because the physical walls are non-porous and impermeable, the streamfunction is constant along the entire perimeter ($\psi|_{\partial \Omega} = 0$). Boundary conditions for vorticity $\omega$ are derived from the kinematic no-slip condition using a Taylor expansion into the interior domain normal to the wall (Thom's boundary closure~\cite{thom1933flow}):
\begin{align}
    \omega_{\text{bottom}}(x) &= -\frac{2 \psi(x, h)}{h^2}, \\
    \omega_{\text{left}}(y)   &= -\frac{2 \psi(h, y)}{h^2}, \\
    \omega_{\text{right}}(y)  &= -\frac{2 \psi(1-h, y)}{h^2}, \\
    \omega_{\text{top}}(x)    &= -\frac{2 \psi(x, 1-h)}{h^2} - \frac{2 U_{\text{lid}}(x)}{h}.
\end{align}
Interior discretizations maintain formal second-order spatial accuracy $\mathcal{O}(h^2)$. At the solid boundaries, however, Thom's boundary closure introduces an $\mathcal{O}(h)$ local truncation error for boundary vorticity, which sets the asymptotic rate of convergence for the coupled streamfunction--vorticity system~\cite{thom1933flow,roache1998verification}. 

At the singular driving corners $(0, 1)$ and $(1, 1)$, horizontal velocity drops discontinuously from the moving lid ($u_{\text{lid}} = 1$) to the stationary sidewall ($u = 0$), generating theoretically singular, non-integrable corner vorticity and stress concentrations~\cite{botella1998benchmark,bruneau20062d}. In the discrete streamfunction--vorticity formulation, tangential velocity is set to zero on the stationary wall, and the four corner vorticity values are evaluated from arithmetic means of their two adjacent boundary neighbors:
\begin{align}
    \omega_{0, 0} &= \frac{1}{2}(\omega_{1, 0} + \omega_{0, 1}) \quad \text{(bottom-left)}, \\
    \omega_{0, N-1} &= \frac{1}{2}(\omega_{1, N-1} + \omega_{0, N-2}) \quad \text{(bottom-right)}, \\
    \omega_{N-1, 0} &= \frac{1}{2}(\omega_{N-2, 0} + \omega_{N-1, 1}) \quad \text{(top-left)}, \\
    \omega_{N-1, N-1} &= \frac{1}{2}(\omega_{N-2, N-1} + \omega_{N-1, N-2}) \quad \text{(top-right)},
\end{align}
where top-lid boundary points $\omega_{N-1, 1}$ and $\omega_{N-1, N-2}$ incorporate the lid driving term $-2 U_{\text{lid}} / h$ from Thom's formula; sidewall boundary points do not.

Under-relaxation stabilizes iterative wall updates during high-$Re$ continuation:
\begin{equation}
    \omega_{\text{wall}}^{(k+1)} = (1 - \beta_{\text{wall}}) \omega_{\text{wall}}^{(k)} + \beta_{\text{wall}} \omega_{\text{Thom}}^{(k+1)}, \quad 0 < \beta_{\text{wall}} \le 1.
\end{equation}
Moderate Reynolds numbers ($Re \le 25{,}000$) require no relaxation ($\beta_{\text{wall}} = 1.0$), satisfying Thom's algebraic boundary relation directly. At $Re = 50{,}000$ and $Re = 100{,}000$, unrelaxed iterations diverge due to corner shock dispersion on uniform meshes. Setting $\beta_{\text{wall}} = 0.85$ and $0.75$, respectively, introduces a discrete temporal low-pass filter on boundary vorticity:
\begin{equation}
    \omega_{\text{wall}}^{n+1} = (1 - \beta_{\text{wall}}) \omega_{\text{wall}}^n + \beta_{\text{wall}} \omega_{\text{Thom}}^{n+1}.
\end{equation}
This dampens parasitic high-wavenumber modes triggered by the corner velocity jump. Unsteady solutions obtained under $\beta_{\text{wall}} < 1.0$ therefore govern a \emph{filtered-boundary model}, whose spectral peaks and shedding frequencies reflect this boundary regularization.

\section{Numerical Architecture \& High-Performance Algorithms}

\subsection{Fast Discrete Sine Transform (DST) Poisson Inversion}
The discrete streamfunction Poisson equation (\ref{eq:poisson_psi}) on a uniform $N \times N$ mesh ($h = 1/(N-1)$) with homogeneous Dirichlet conditions $\psi|_{\partial \Omega} = 0$ represents an $(N-2)^2 \times (N-2)^2$ linear system. The 5-point discrete Laplacian on a Cartesian grid admits analytical sinusoidal eigenmodes:
\begin{equation}
    \phi_{j, m} = \sin\left(\frac{j m \pi}{N-1}\right), \quad j, m = 1, \dots, N-2.
\end{equation}
Because these modes match the Type-I Discrete Sine Transform (DST-I), exact algebraic diagonalization is possible~\cite{swarztrauber1977methods}. Applying the 2D DST-I to both sides of $\nabla_h^2 \psi = -\omega$ yields:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\psi}_{k_x, k_y} = \frac{\hat{\omega}_{k_x, k_y}}{\lambda_{k_x} + \lambda_{k_y}}, \quad \lambda_k = \frac{4}{h^2} \sin^2\left(\frac{k \pi}{2(N-1)}\right).
\end{equation}
Inverting back to physical space via the 2D inverse DST-I recovers $\psi_{i, j}$ to machine precision ($\sim 10^{-16}$) in $\mathcal{O}(N^2 \log N)$ operations (\texttt{scipy.fft.dstn}). Iterative residual drift is bypassed entirely. On an Intel Core i7-12700H (Python 3.11, SciPy 1.11 single-threaded PocketFFT backend), one spectral inversion executes in $0.8\,\text{ms}$ on $257 \times 257$ and $2.1\,\text{ms}$ on $513 \times 513$.

\subsection{Vectorized Alternating Direction Implicit (ADI) Marching}
Vorticity transport (\ref{eq:vorticity_transport}) is marched via operator-split Alternating Direction Implicit (ADI) integration based on the classical Peaceman--Rachford structure~\cite{peaceman1955numerical}. To decouple coordinates while preserving high-throughput array operations, advective fluxes $\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{u}^n, \omega^n) = u^n \delta_x \omega^n + v^n \delta_y \omega^n$ are evaluated explicitly at time level $n$. Viscous diffusion is split implicitly across two half-steps of length $\Delta t / 2$:
\begin{align}
    \frac{\omega^* - \omega^n}{\Delta t / 2} - \frac{1}{Re}\delta_x^2 \omega^* &= \frac{1}{Re}\delta_y^2 \omega^n - \left( u^n \delta_x \omega^n + v^n \delta_y \omega^n \right), \label{eq:adi_step1} \\
    \frac{\omega^{n+1} - \omega^*}{\Delta t / 2} - \frac{1}{Re}\delta_y^2 \omega^{n+1} &= \frac{1}{Re}\delta_x^2 \omega^* - \left( u^n \delta_x \omega^n + v^n \delta_y \omega^n \right), \label{eq:adi_step2}
\end{align}
where $\delta_x^2$ and $\delta_y^2$ represent second-order central Laplacians. For interior flow with frozen advection, the directional split possesses formal temporal accuracy $\mathcal{O}(\Delta t) + \mathcal{O}(h^2)$, alongside unconditional linear diffusive stability. Thom's boundary condition and the high-$Re$ upwind stencil reduce overall spatial accuracy to $\mathcal{O}(h)$.

Because diffusion alone is treated implicitly in each coordinate direction, the tridiagonal systems carry constant scalar coefficients across any given grid line:
\begin{equation}
    A = -\frac{\alpha_{\text{ADI}}}{2}, \quad B = 1 + \alpha_{\text{ADI}}, \quad C = -\frac{\alpha_{\text{ADI}}}{2}, \quad \text{with} \quad \alpha_{\text{ADI}} = \frac{\Delta t}{Re \cdot h^2}.
\end{equation}
Thomas algorithm tridiagonal factorizations~\cite{thomas1949elliptic} $(P, \text{denom})$ need be computed only once per simulation. Entire coordinate blocks ($M = N-2$ rows or columns) are solved simultaneously through vectorized array broadcasting in NumPy. Up to $Re = 50{,}000$, all convective terms use pure second-order central differencing:
\begin{equation}
    (u \delta_x \omega)_{i, j} = u_{i, j} \frac{\omega_{i+1, j} - \omega_{i-1, j}}{2 h}, \quad (v \delta_y \omega)_{i, j} = v_{i, j} \frac{\omega_{i, j+1} - \omega_{i, j-1}}{2 h}.
\end{equation}
Artificial numerical diffusion is strictly zero ($\nu_{\text{art}} = 0$) throughout this regime.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Cell P\'eclet Number Scaling and Hybrid Regularization Model ($Re = 100,000$)}{Cell Peclet Number Scaling and Hybrid Regularization Model (Re = 100000)}}
In finite-difference discretizations of convection--diffusion equations, the local cell P\'eclet number governs the spatial monotonicity of central differences. We define the signed cell P\'eclet components and their absolute magnitudes:
\begin{align}
    \widetilde{Pe}_{x, i, j} &= \frac{u_{i, j} h}{\nu} = Re \cdot u_{i, j} \cdot h, \quad Pe_{x, i, j} = |\widetilde{Pe}_{x, i, j}|, \\
    \widetilde{Pe}_{y, i, j} &= \frac{v_{i, j} h}{\nu} = Re \cdot v_{i, j} \cdot h, \quad Pe_{y, i, j} = |\widetilde{Pe}_{y, i, j}|,
\end{align}
with directional maximum $Pe_{h, i, j} = \max(Pe_{x, i, j}, Pe_{y, i, j})$.

In classical numerical analysis of linear convection--diffusion equations, the discrete maximum principle (or M-matrix property) dictates that central differencing yields monotonic, oscillation-free solutions if and only if the cell P\'eclet number satisfies the well-known threshold $Pe_h \le 2$~\cite{patankar1980numerical,spalding1972novel,roache1998verification,ferziger2002computational}. For the 1D model problem $u \, d\phi/dx = \nu \, d^2\phi/dx^2$, the three-point central-difference algebraic equation is $(1 + Pe_h/2)\phi_{i-1} - 2\phi_i + (1 - Pe_h/2)\phi_{i+1} = 0$, where $Pe_h \equiv |u|h/\nu$. To guarantee non-negative off-diagonal matrix coefficients and preclude non-physical spatial extrema, the downstream stencil coefficient requires $(1 - Pe_h/2) \ge 0$, establishing $Pe_h \le 2$. When $Pe_h > 2$, the negative neighbor weighting violates the maximum principle, generating spurious, high-wavenumber node-to-node oscillations commonly termed "grid wiggles" in the presence of steep gradients~\cite{patankar1980numerical,roache1998verification,ferziger2002computational}. On a $513 \times 513$ mesh ($h = 1/512 \approx 1.953 \times 10^{-3}$) driven by a unit-speed lid, the nominal maximum cell P\'eclet number scales directly with Reynolds number:
\begin{align}
    Re = 1{,}000  &\implies Pe \approx 1.95 < 2 \quad \text{(monotonic central stencil)}, \notag \\
    Re = 10{,}000 &\implies Pe \approx 19.53, \notag \\
    Re = 50{,}000 &\implies Pe \approx 97.66, \notag \\
    Re = 100{,}000 &\implies Pe \approx 195.31 \gg 2.
\end{align}
Concurrently, according to classical laminar boundary layer theory~\cite{prandtl1904uber,schlichting2000boundary}, the viscous wall shear layer thins asymptotically as $\delta \sim L / \sqrt{Re}$. At $Re = 100{,}000$, $\delta \approx 3.16 \times 10^{-3} L$. The uniform $513 \times 513$ grid resolves this layer with only $\delta / h \approx 1.62$ cells. At the lid corners $(0, 1)$ and $(1, 1)$, velocity jumps discontinuously from $u_{\text{lid}} = 1$ to $u_{\text{wall}} = 0$, driving $\partial \omega / \partial x \to \infty$~\cite{botella1998benchmark,bruneau20062d}. Under $Pe_h \approx 195.3$, pure central differences generate parasitic high-frequency waves along the boundary that trigger divergence on uniform Cartesian meshes.

To regularize these dispersive corner transients at $Re = 100{,}000$, we adopt the classical hybrid differencing philosophy of Spalding~\cite{spalding1972novel} and Patankar~\cite{patankar1980numerical}, switching directionally from second-order central differencing to first-order upwinding precisely where the local signed cell P\'eclet number exceeds two:
\begin{equation}
    (u \delta_x \omega)_{i, j} = \begin{cases}
        u_{i, j} \frac{\omega_{i+1, j} - \omega_{i-1, j}}{2 h}, & |\widetilde{Pe}_{x, i, j}| \le 2, \\
        u_{i, j} \frac{\omega_{i, j} - \omega_{i-1, j}}{h}, & \widetilde{Pe}_{x, i, j} > 2, \\
        u_{i, j} \frac{\omega_{i+1, j} - \omega_{i, j}}{h}, & \widetilde{Pe}_{x, i, j} < -2,
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
with an identical formulation along $y$. Implicit ADI sweeps retain full physical viscosity $(1/Re)\nabla^2 \omega$. Upwinding adds directional numerical diffusion $\boldsymbol{\nu}_{\text{art}} = \text{diag}(\nu_{\text{art}, x}, \nu_{\text{art}, y})$:
\begin{equation}
    \nu_{\text{art}, x} = \frac{|u_{i, j}| h}{2} \mathbf{1}_{Pe_x > 2}, \quad \nu_{\text{art}, y} = \frac{|v_{i, j}| h}{2} \mathbf{1}_{Pe_y > 2}.
\end{equation}
The effective viscosity is $\nu_{\text{eff}} = \nu + \nu_{\text{art}}$. Because velocities vary spatially, this dissipation is anisotropic. Near the cavity center $(0.5, 0.5)$, velocities drop to $u \approx -0.0304, v \approx -0.0036$, yielding $Pe_x \approx 5.94$ and $Pe_y \approx 0.70 < 2$. Only the horizontal coordinate is upwinded here ($\nu_{\text{art}, x} / \nu = 2.97$, $\nu_{\text{art}, y} = 0$). Taking the mean directional ratio:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\bar{\nu}_{\text{art}}}{\nu} = \frac{1}{2}\left( \frac{\nu_{\text{art}, x}}{\nu} + \frac{\nu_{\text{art}, y}}{\nu} \right) = \frac{1}{4} \left[ Pe_x \mathbf{1}_{Pe_x > 2} + Pe_y \mathbf{1}_{Pe_y > 2} \right], \label{eq:excess_visc}
\end{equation}
gives $\bar{\nu}_{\text{art}} / \nu = 1.485$ at the core center. The active interior maximum reaches $\bar{\nu}_{\text{art}} / \nu = 48.93$ at $y = 1-h = 0.99805$ (nominal lid boundary limit $48.83$), and the domain average is $\langle \bar{\nu}_{\text{art}} / \nu \rangle = 16.32$.

Figure~\ref{fig:peclet_dissipation_map} audits this dissipation field. On the $513 \times 513$ mesh, pure central differencing ($Pe \le 2, \bar{\nu}_{\text{art}} = 0$) governs $1.79\%$ of the area, confined to stagnation zones and lines of zero velocity. Across the remaining $98.21\%$, directional upwinding provides the stabilization necessary to prevent corner shock breakdown. The added viscosity is concentrated near the moving lid ($Pe_{\max} = 195.31$) and decays steeply toward the core, as seen in Figure~\ref{fig:peclet_dissipation_map}(c). Refining the grid to $1025 \times 1025$ ($h = 1/1024$) halves the cell spacing: the central differencing domain widens to $3.09\%$, peak active interior dissipation drops to $\bar{\nu}_{\text{art}} / \nu \le 24.46$, core dissipation falls to $0.74\nu$, and domain-averaged added viscosity drops to $8.12\nu$. 
The $Re = 100{,}000$ calculation is thus an explicitly regularized numerical model rather than an unadulterated discretization of the Navier--Stokes equations. For all lower Reynolds numbers ($Re \le 50{,}000$), by contrast, central differencing operates over $100\%$ of the domain with $\nu_{\text{art}} = 0$ identically.

\subsection{Reynolds Number Homotopy Continuation Ladder}
Impulsive starts from quiescent initial conditions ($\mathbf{u} = 0, \psi = 0$) fail at high Reynolds numbers ($Re \ge 5{,}000$). The sudden acceleration creates sharp corner boundary layer detachments that provoke numerical instability in central difference operators. We circumvent this startup shock with a parametric continuation ladder:
\begin{align}
    \mathcal{H}(Re) : 100 &\longrightarrow 1,000 \longrightarrow 3,200 \longrightarrow 5,000 \longrightarrow 7,500 \longrightarrow 10,000 \notag \\
    &\longrightarrow 15,000 \longrightarrow 20,000 \longrightarrow 25,000 \longrightarrow 30,000 \longrightarrow 35,000 \notag \\
    &\longrightarrow 40,000 \longrightarrow 45,000 \longrightarrow 50,000 \longrightarrow 100,000.
\end{align}
Each stage $Re_m$ provides the converged initial condition for step $Re_{m+1}$. When switching mesh densities, bicubic spline interpolation projects coarse-grid solutions onto finer Cartesian grids followed by boundary re-normalization.

\section{Stationary Continuation Solutions and Benchmark Comparisons (\texorpdfstring{$Re = 100 \to 100{,}000$}{Re = 100 to 100000})}

\subsection{Primary and Secondary Eddy Benchmark Comparison}
Computed flow states are compared against the canonical benchmark of Ghia et al.~\cite{ghia1982high} and the high-Reynolds studies of Erturk et al.~\cite{erturk2005numerical,erturk2009discussions}. Beyond the critical threshold ($Re_{\text{cr}} \approx 8{,}000$), two-dimensional cavity flow becomes physically unstable to time-dependent oscillations~\cite{shen1991hopf,peng2003transition,bruneau20062d}. In time-accurate unsteady marching, infinitesimal disturbances grow along linearly unstable eigenmodes. For steady continuation, however, following the pseudo-transient approach of Erturk et al.~\cite{erturk2005numerical,erturk2009discussions}, we track the stationary fixed point of the discrete equations by pairing pseudo-transient ADI iterations with parametric homotopy. Boundary under-relaxation ($\beta_{\text{wall}} \le 1.0$) and directional splitting act as numerical dampers on physical oscillations, stabilizing the discrete iteration matrix and steering the solver along the steady continuation branch. Iterations proceed until the maximum interior vorticity increment drops below $\|\omega^{k+1} - \omega^k\|_\infty < 10^{-5}$. \subsection{\texorpdfstring{Core Vorticity Homogenization \& Batchelor's Hypothesis ($Re \to 100,000$)}{Core Vorticity Homogenization and Batchelor's Hypothesis (Re -> 100,000)}}
Two-dimensional flows with closed streamlines tend toward spatial vorticity homogenization at high Reynolds numbers. Batchelor's asymptotic theorem~\cite{batchelor1956steady} establishes that in the limit of vanishing viscosity ($Re \to \infty$), any steady two-dimensional Navier--Stokes state containing closed streamlines must attain a spatially uniform vorticity $\omega(x, y) = \omega_0 = \text{const}$ throughout the inviscid recirculating core ($\lim_{Re \to \infty} \nabla \omega = 0$ for $(x, y) \in \Omega_{\text{core}}$), as convective circulation along closed orbits balances cross-stream diffusion.
\begin{equation}
    \lim_{Re \to \infty} \nabla \omega(x, y) = 0 \quad \text{for } (x, y) \in \Omega_{\text{core}}.
\end{equation}
The theorem dictates spatial uniformity, not a universal numeric value for $\omega_0$. By coupling the inviscid core to an integral momentum balance across thin laminar wall boundary layers, Burggraf~\cite{burggraf1966analytical} predicted $\omega_0 \approx -1.886$ (under the clockwise sign convention) for an idealized square cavity under the restrictive assumptions of unseparated wall boundary layers and absence of secondary corner recirculations. We evaluate core vorticity by averaging over $y \in [0.35, 0.65]$ along the vertical mid-plane $x = 0.5$:
\begin{equation}
    \omega_0 = \frac{1}{0.30} \int_{0.35}^{0.65} \omega(0.5, y) \, dy.
\end{equation}
Across our continuation branches, the core vorticity settles at $\omega_0 = -2.5719$ ($Re = 10{,}000$), $-2.5453$ ($Re = 50{,}000$), and $-2.5418$ ($Re = 100{,}000$), with standard deviation $\approx 0.10$ across the span. Vorticity gradients flatten as predicted by Batchelor ($\nabla \omega \approx 0$). However, while Batchelor's theorem assumes unregularized steady Navier--Stokes dynamics, our computations at $Re \ge 50{,}000$ employ boundary filtering ($\beta_{\text{wall}} < 1.0$) and, at $Re = 100{,}000$, directional hybrid regularization. Furthermore, extensive corner vortex systems (Table~\ref{tab:corner_vortices}) displace the primary boundary layers away from the solid walls. The departure from Burggraf's $-1.886$ limit reflects the physical presence of these detached corner eddies alongside the mathematical properties of the regularized model.

As wall boundary layers contract and corner eddies establish topological equilibrium, the primary vortex core shifts toward the geometric mid-plane ($x_c \to 0.50000$ at $Re = 50{,}000$, and $0.4990$ on $1025 \times 1025$). The core minimum streamfunction stabilizes at $\psi_{\min} \approx -0.12572$.

\subsection{Formal Grid Convergence Index (GCI) and Richardson Extrapolation}
We quantify spatial discretization uncertainty following the ASME V\&V 20-2009 standard~\cite{asme2009standard} and Roache's Grid Convergence Index (GCI) framework~\cite{roache1994perspective,roache1997quantification,roache1998verification}. Two three-grid triplets with constant refinement ratio $r = h_{\text{coarse}} / h_{\text{fine}} = 2.0$ are evaluated:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Triplet 1 ($Re = 10,000$):} $N_1 = 513 \times 513$ ($h_1 = 1/512$), $N_2 = 257 \times 257$ ($h_2 = 1/256$), $N_3 = 129 \times 129$ ($h_3 = 1/128$).
    \item \textbf{Triplet 2 ($Re = 100,000$):} $N_1 = 1025 \times 1025$ ($h_1 = 1/1024$), $N_2 = 513 \times 513$ ($h_2 = 1/512$), $N_3 = 257 \times 257$ ($h_3 = 1/256$).
\end{itemize}

Observed convergence order $p$, Richardson-extrapolated continuum value $f_{h=0}$, relative errors $e_a^{21} = |(f_1 - f_2)/f_1|$ and $e_a^{32} = |(f_2 - f_3)/f_2|$, and fine-grid GCI with safety factor $F_s = 1.25$ follow standard definitions:
\begin{equation}
    p = \frac{\ln |(f_3 - f_2) / (f_2 - f_1)|}{\ln(r)}, \quad f_{h=0} = f_1 + \frac{f_1 - f_2}{r^p - 1}, \quad \text{GCI}_{21} = \frac{F_s \cdot e_a^{21}}{r^p - 1}, \quad \text{GCI}_{32} = \frac{F_s \cdot e_a^{32} \cdot r^p}{r^p - 1}.
\end{equation}

\section{Finite-Window Unsteady Dynamics and Vortex Shedding}

\subsection{Unsteady Vortex Shedding on \texorpdfstring{$513 \times 513$}{513x513} (\texorpdfstring{$Re = 10,000$}{Re = 10000})}
Under the two-dimensional incompressible Navier--Stokes equations, lid-driven cavity flow undergoes a supercritical Hopf bifurcation as the Reynolds number crosses a critical threshold $Re_{\text{cr}}$~\cite{shen1991hopf,peng2003transition,bruneau20062d,auteri2002numerical}. Regularized lid models with smooth polynomial velocity profiles place this bifurcation near $Re_{\text{cr}} \approx 7{,}400$ ($St \approx 0.59$)~\cite{peng2003transition,shen1991hopf}. In contrast, solvers retaining discontinuous corner velocities place the critical threshold between $Re = 8{,}000$ and $8{,}050$ ($St \approx 0.45$ at $Re = 8{,}100$)~\cite{bruneau20062d,auteri2002numerical}. We reiterate that in physical three-dimensional experiments and 3D direct numerical simulations, the flow becomes three-dimensional and unsteady at substantially lower Reynolds numbers ($Re \sim 1{,}000\text{--}3{,}200$) due to centrifugal Taylor--G\"ortler instabilities~\cite{koseff1984hydrothermal,aidun1991primary,albensoeder2001three}. The present investigation strictly addresses the canonical two-dimensional mathematical model, where spanwise perturbations are constrained to zero.

We probe this post-bifurcation periodic state at $Re = 10{,}000$ on the $513 \times 513$ mesh without boundary relaxation ($\beta_{\text{wall}} = 1.0$). The simulation runs for $44{,}000$ physical time steps ($\Delta t = 5.0 \times 10^{-4}$ convective time units, reaching $t = 22.0$, $CFL \approx 0.256$). To trigger the unstable mode, a Gaussian perturbation $\delta \omega = 0.05 \exp(-[(x-0.8)^2 + (y-0.8)^2]/0.05^2)$ was seeded in the upper shear layer. Telemetry probes logged velocity signals every 10 steps ($\Delta t_{\text{sample}} = 0.005$) inside the bottom-left detachment region $(0.08, 0.15)$.

\subsection{Diagnostic Pressure Poisson Formulation and Discrete Solver}
\label{sec:pressure_reconstruction}
To reconstruct the scalar pressure field $p(x, y)$ from the streamfunction--vorticity solution in post-processing, we formulate the pressure Poisson equation derived from the divergence of the 2D incompressible Navier--Stokes momentum equations~\cite{gresho1987solving,quartapelle1993numerical}:
\begin{equation}
    \nabla^2 p = 2 \left[ \left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}\right)\left(\frac{\partial v}{\partial y}\right) - \left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\right)\left(\frac{\partial v}{\partial x}\right) \right] = - \left[ \left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}\right)^2 + 2\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\frac{\partial v}{\partial x} + \left(\frac{\partial v}{\partial y}\right)^2 \right], \label{eq:pressure_poisson}
\end{equation}
under the assumption of incompressibility $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} = 0$. In our implementation (\path{lid_driven_cavity_fdm.py}), equation~(\ref{eq:pressure_poisson}) is discretized on the uniform Cartesian mesh ($h = \Delta x = \Delta y$) using the standard discrete 5-point Laplacian stencil for interior nodes ($1 \le i, j \le N-2$):
\begin{equation}
    \nabla_h^2 p_{i, j} \equiv \frac{p_{i+1, j} + p_{i-1, j} + p_{i, j+1} + p_{i, j-1} - 4 p_{i, j}}{h^2} = S_{i, j},
\end{equation}
where the discrete source term $S_{i, j}$ is evaluated using second-order central differences applied to the reconstructed velocity components:
\begin{equation}
    S_{i, j} = - \left[ \left(\delta_x u_{i, j}\right)^2 + 2 (\delta_y u_{i, j}) (\delta_x v_{i, j}) + \left(\delta_y v_{i, j}\right)^2 \right],
\end{equation}
with $\delta_x \phi_{i, j} = (\phi_{i+1, j} - \phi_{i-1, j})/(2h)$ and $\delta_y \phi_{i, j} = (\phi_{i, j+1} - \phi_{i, j-1})/(2h)$.

Because velocity boundary conditions are enforced on the cavity walls, momentum-consistent wall pressure boundary conditions derived from the normal momentum equation require evaluating boundary-normal viscous diffusion and convective projections $\partial p / \partial n = \mathbf{n} \cdot (\frac{1}{Re}\nabla^2 \mathbf{u} - (\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u})$~\cite{gresho1987solving,quartapelle1993numerical}. As a simplified diagnostic surrogate for flow topology visualization, the solver applies discrete homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions via first-order ghost-node copying:
\begin{equation}
    p_{0, j} = p_{1, j}, \quad p_{N-1, j} = p_{N-2, j}, \quad p_{i, 0} = p_{i, 1}, \quad p_{i, N-1} = p_{i, N-2}.
\end{equation}
The discrete system is advanced via Jacobi relaxation:
\begin{equation}
    p_{i, j}^{(k+1)} = \frac{1}{4} \left( p_{i+1, j}^{(k)} + p_{i-1, j}^{(k)} + p_{i, j+1}^{(k)} + p_{i, j-1}^{(k)} - h^2 S_{i, j} \right),
\end{equation}
until the maximum step increment satisfies $\max_{1 \le i, j \le N-2} |p_{i, j}^{(k+1)} - p_{i, j}^{(k)}| < 10^{-5}$ (or a maximum of $2500$ iterations). To fix the arbitrary additive pressure constant resulting from the pure Neumann boundary condition, a zero-mean gauge is projected across the interior domain nodes:
\begin{equation}
    p_{i, j} \leftarrow p_{i, j} - \frac{1}{(N-2)^2} \sum_{k=1}^{N-2} \sum_{m=1}^{N-2} p_{k, m}.
\end{equation}
This diagnostic scalar field serves for qualitative flow topology visualization. Users requiring momentum-consistent wall pressure distributions should note that the diagnostic Neumann boundary condition neglects boundary-normal viscous diffusion terms.

\section{Conclusion}
This work introduced an open-source, high-resolution finite-difference framework for two-dimensional lid-driven cavity flows, computing stationary continuation solutions up to $Re = 100{,}000$ on uniform Cartesian meshes reaching $1025 \times 1025$ nodes ($1{,}050{,}625$ points) and analyzing finite-window unsteady dynamics on $513 \times 513$ meshes ($263{,}169$ points). By combining a direct $\mathcal{O}(N^2 \log N)$ Discrete Sine Transform (DST-I) Poisson solver with second-order central differencing and continuation warm-starts, the framework executes with zero added numerical diffusion ($\nu_{\text{art}} = 0$) up to $Re = 50{,}000$. At $Re = 100{,}000$, where cell P\'eclet numbers exceed 195, interior directional upwinding stabilizes singular corner shocks under an explicitly characterized hybrid-regularized model.

Geometric comparisons against canonical benchmarks confirm four-decimal core position agreement at $Re = 100$ and $1{,}000$. At $Re = 25{,}000$, primary core position matches Erturk's non-uniform compact solution within $\Delta = 0.00688$. Across high Reynolds numbers ($Re = 50{,}000$ and $100{,}000$), the primary recirculating core approaches geometric mid-plane symmetry ($x_c \to 0.50000$ on $513 \times 513$ and $x_c = 0.4990$ on $1025 \times 1025$), exhibiting behavior consistent with Batchelor's core homogenization hypothesis within the regularized model ($\bar{\omega}_0 \approx -2.54$). Time-accurate simulations capture periodic shedding at $Re = 10{,}000$ ($St = 0.6249 \pm 0.0416$), an amplitude-modulated multi-frequency state at $Re = 15{,}000$ ($St = 0.5364 \pm 0.0278$), and nonlinear multi-loop shedding at $Re = 25{,}000$ ($St = 0.0953 \pm 0.0281$), $Re = 50{,}000$ ($St = 0.2581 \pm 0.0300$), and $Re = 100{,}000$ ($St = 0.1908 \pm 0.0379$). For $Re \ge 50{,}000$, boundary under-relaxation acts as a temporal filter, defining a filtered-boundary model.

Releasing all thirty verified datasets, solver implementations, and figure reproduction pipelines under the MIT license establishes an auditable open benchmark for fluid dynamics algorithms.
