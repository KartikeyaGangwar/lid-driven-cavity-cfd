\begin{abstract}
The 2D incompressible lid-driven cavity problem serves as a canonical benchmark in computational fluid dynamics. While classical benchmarks (such as Ghia et al., 1982) are largely restricted to $Re \le 10{,}000$, existing higher-$Re$ investigations either focus exclusively on steady solutions ($Re \le 30{,}000$) or omit open full-field tensor archives and unified time-accurate bifurcation records up to extreme Reynolds numbers ($Re = 100{,}000$). We present a high-resolution finite-difference framework in streamfunction--vorticity formulation across steady continuation and unsteady regimes from $Re = 100$ to $Re = 100{,}000$. The numerical engine integrates an $\mathcal{O}(N^2 \log N)$ Discrete Sine Transform (DST-I) Poisson solver, eliminating iterative Poisson residuals, coupled with vectorized Alternating Direction Implicit (ADI) marching and parameter homotopy continuation. Across $Re \le 50{,}000$, the spatial discretization operates strictly with pure second-order central differencing ($\nu_{\text{art}} = 0$). At $Re = 100{,}000$ (peak directional cell P\'eclet number $Pe_x \approx 195.3$), an interior hybrid upwind stabilization regularizes corner singularity dispersion with added numerical viscosity ratio $\bar{\nu}_{\text{art}}/\nu = \frac{1}{4}[Pe_x \mathbf{1}_{Pe_x > 2} + Pe_y \mathbf{1}_{Pe_y > 2}]$, yielding $1.485$ at the core and $48.83$ at the lid on $513 \times 513$ (dropping to $0.74$ and $24.46$ on $1025 \times 1025$). On uniform meshes up to $1025 \times 1025$, stationary continuation resolves primary and secondary corner eddies, demonstrating geometric agreement with reference benchmarks while documenting a consistent $\sim 7.5\%$ offset in $\psi_{\min}$. At $Re = 50{,}000$ and $Re = 100{,}000$, the core vorticity exhibits behavior qualitatively consistent with Batchelor's asymptotic homogenization hypothesis ($\omega_0 \approx -2.54$) with mid-plane centering ($x_c \to 0.50000$). Physical time marching on $513 \times 513$ grids over extended windows ($t = 16.0$--$22.0$ convective time units, with Strouhal number $St \equiv f L / U$) resolves periodic limit-cycle dynamics at $Re = 10{,}000$ ($St = 0.6249 \pm 0.0416$), captures an amplitude-modulated multi-frequency response at $Re = 15{,}000$ ($St = 0.5364 \pm 0.0278$), and characterizes nonlinear shedding at $Re = 25{,}000$ ($St = 0.0953 \pm 0.0281$), $Re = 50{,}000$ ($St = 0.2581 \pm 0.0300$), and $Re = 100{,}000$ ($St = 0.1908 \pm 0.0379$). All datasets and automated pipelines are openly archived.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The incompressible flow inside a two-dimensional square cavity driven by a translating lid has been the canonical proving ground for numerical fluid algorithms for more than four decades~\cite{ghia1982high,erturk2005numerical,bruneau20062d,hou1995lattice}. Despite the simplicity of its rectangular geometry and boundary closures, the enclosed fluid exhibits rich, nonlinear physical phenomena: a dominant central gyre, secondary counter-rotating corner eddies, viscous wall boundary layers, singular velocity discontinuities at top corners, and hydrodynamic instability leading to Hopf bifurcations and chaotic transitions~\cite{peng2003transition}.

In computational fluid dynamics and high-order algorithm development, the lid-driven cavity problem is widely recognized as the preeminent standard for verifying spatial discretization schemes, benchmarking Poisson solvers, and investigating nonlinear hydrodynamic stability. However, computational engineering researchers facing this problem frequently confront severe data limitations:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Sparsity of Benchmark Data:} The canonical benchmark of Ghia, Ghia \& Shin (1982)~\cite{ghia1982high} provides velocity data along only two 1D orthogonal lines (centerlines) sampled at 17 discrete points for $Re \le 10,000$. Full 2D coordinate fields for velocity, vorticity, and pressure are rarely tabulated or accessible in modern tensor formats.
    \item \textbf{Artificial Numerical Dissipation:} At high Reynolds numbers ($Re \ge 3,200$), cell P\'eclet numbers ($Pe = u h / \nu$) substantially exceed the classical linear stability limit of $Pe = 2$. To prevent unphysical spatial oscillations (wiggles) and catastrophic divergence, commercial packages and naive implementations routinely inject first-order upwind differencing or flux limiters, which introduce substantial numerical viscosity ($\nu_{\text{num}} \sim \mathcal{O}(h)$), artificially damping corner eddies and distorting core vortex dynamics.
    \item \textbf{Setup Friction and Closed Access:} Existing high-$Re$ benchmarks (e.g., Erturk et al., 2005~\cite{erturk2005numerical}; Erturk, 2009~\cite{erturk2009discussions}) were implemented in proprietary or legacy Fortran codes without standardized open-source distribution. Consequently, reproducing solutions requires significant manual re-implementation.
\end{enumerate}

To bridge this gap, this work develops and documents an open-source finite-difference solver built entirely in modern scientific Python (NumPy, SciPy). Operating with \emph{pure second-order central differencing} ($\nu_{\text{num}} = 0$) across moderate-to-high Reynolds regimes and an interior hybrid upwind stabilization for extreme Reynolds numbers ($Re = 100,000$), the solver leverages a spectral Poisson solver based on the Type-I Discrete Sine Transform (DST-I), a vectorized Alternating Direction Implicit (ADI) time-marching scheme, and parameter continuation (homotopy) across Reynolds numbers. We systematically evaluate stationary continuation solutions from $Re = 100$ to $Re = 100,000$ on meshes up to $1025 \times 1025$ ($1,050,625$ nodes), provide a grid convergence verification, and perform time-accurate investigations of vortex shedding and limit-cycle dynamics on $513 \times 513$ grids.

All computational flow fields, post-processing tools, and verification datasets are archived permanently under an open-source MIT license on GitHub\footnote{\url{https://github.com/KartikeyaGangwar/lid-driven-cavity-cfd}} and Zenodo~\cite{singh2026solver}\footnote{\url{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.18312938}}.

\section{Governing Equations \& Mathematical Formulation}

\subsection{Streamfunction--Vorticity System}
For 2D incompressible Newtonian fluid flow in a domain $\Omega = [0, L] \times [0, L]$, the Navier--Stokes equations in primitive variables $(\mathbf{u}, p)$ are:
\begin{align}
    \nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} &= 0, \\
    \frac{\partial \mathbf{u}}{\partial t} + (\mathbf{u} \cdot \nabla)\mathbf{u} &= -\frac{1}{\rho}\nabla p + \nu \nabla^2 \mathbf{u}.
\end{align}
Defining the non-dimensionalization based on cavity width $L$ and lid velocity $U$:
\begin{equation}
    x^* = \frac{x}{L}, \quad y^* = \frac{y}{L}, \quad \mathbf{u}^* = \frac{\mathbf{u}}{U}, \quad t^* = \frac{t U}{L}, \quad Re = \frac{U L}{\nu}.
\end{equation}
Here $t^*$ measures non-dimensional physical time in convective time units (c.t.u.). Correspondingly, oscillatory frequencies $f$ are non-dimensionalized as the Strouhal number $St \equiv f L / U$. Taking the curl of the momentum equation, pressure is eliminated. Introducing the streamfunction $\psi$ and scalar vorticity $\omega$:
\begin{equation}
    u = \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial y}, \quad v = -\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial x}, \quad \omega = \frac{\partial v}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial u}{\partial y},
\end{equation}
incompressibility is satisfied identically ($\nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} = \psi_{yx} - \psi_{xy} = 0$). The non-dimensional governing system becomes (omitting asterisks for clarity):
\begin{align}
    \frac{\partial \omega}{\partial t} + u \frac{\partial \omega}{\partial x} + v \frac{\partial \omega}{\partial y} &= \frac{1}{Re} \left( \frac{\partial^2 \omega}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2 \omega}{\partial y^2} \right), \label{eq:vorticity_transport} \\
    \nabla^2 \psi &= -\omega. \label{eq:poisson_psi}
\end{align}

\subsection{Boundary Closures \& Wall Vorticity}
The physical boundary conditions for the unit square cavity are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Bottom wall ($y = 0$):} $u = 0, \quad v = 0 \implies \psi = 0$
    \item \textbf{Left wall ($x = 0$):} $u = 0, \quad v = 0 \implies \psi = 0$
    \item \textbf{Right wall ($x = 1$):} $u = 0, \quad v = 0 \implies \psi = 0$
    \item \textbf{Top moving lid ($y = 1$):} $u = U_{\text{lid}}(x), \quad v = 0 \implies \psi = 0$
\end{itemize}
For standard benchmark comparison with Ghia et al.~\cite{ghia1982high} and Erturk et al.~\cite{erturk2005numerical}, the constant lid profile $U_{\text{lid}}(x) = 1.0$ is employed.

Because the physical walls are non-porous and impermeable, the streamfunction is constant along the entire perimeter ($\psi|_{\partial \Omega} = 0$). Boundary conditions for vorticity $\omega$ are derived from the kinematic no-slip condition using a Taylor expansion into the interior domain normal to the wall (Thom's boundary closure~\cite{thom1933flow}):
\begin{align}
    \omega_{\text{bottom}}(x) &= -\frac{2 \psi(x, h)}{h^2}, \\
    \omega_{\text{left}}(y)   &= -\frac{2 \psi(h, y)}{h^2}, \\
    \omega_{\text{right}}(y)  &= -\frac{2 \psi(1-h, y)}{h^2}, \\
    \omega_{\text{top}}(x)    &= -\frac{2 \psi(x, 1-h)}{h^2} - \frac{2 U_{\text{lid}}(x)}{h}.
\end{align}
It should be noted that while the interior discretization is formally second-order $\mathcal{O}(h^2)$, Thom's boundary formulation has a local truncation error of $\mathcal{O}(h)$ for boundary vorticity, which represents an established and robust closure in streamfunction--vorticity solvers~\cite{thom1933flow,roache1998verification}. At the singular top lid corners $(0, 1)$ and $(1, 1)$, where the horizontal velocity abruptly transitions from $u_{\text{lid}} = 1$ to the stationary sidewall $u = 0$, velocity is assigned to zero on the stationary boundary, and corner vorticity values are evaluated from the adjacent boundary closures. 

To guarantee numerical stability during high-$Re$ iterative coupling, under-relaxation is applied to the wall vorticity updates:
\begin{equation}
    \omega_{\text{wall}}^{(k+1)} = (1 - \beta_{\text{wall}}) \omega_{\text{wall}}^{(k)} + \beta_{\text{wall}} \omega_{\text{Thom}}^{(k+1)}, \quad 0 < \beta_{\text{wall}} \le 1.
\end{equation}
In all time-accurate physical marching and steady continuation at moderate Reynolds numbers ($Re \le 25{,}000$), unrelaxed updates are employed ($\beta_{\text{wall}} = 1.0$), ensuring that the discrete boundary condition is satisfied directly without temporal filtering. At extreme Reynolds numbers ($Re = 50{,}000$ and $Re = 100{,}000$), under-relaxation ($\beta_{\text{wall}} = 0.85$ and $0.75$, respectively) is applied as a numerical boundary regularization to prevent high-wavenumber dispersion generated by corner velocity discontinuities on uniform Cartesian meshes; this regularized boundary condition acts as a localized boundary filter over the reported convective windows.

\section{Numerical Architecture \& High-Performance Algorithms}

\subsection{Fast Discrete Sine Transform (DST) Poisson Inversion}
The discrete 2D streamfunction Poisson equation (\ref{eq:poisson_psi}) on a uniform mesh of size $N \times N$ ($h = 1/(N-1)$) with homogeneous Dirichlet conditions $\psi|_{\partial \Omega} = 0$ represents an $(N-2)^2 \times (N-2)^2$ linear system. Because the 5-point discrete Laplacian on a Cartesian grid possesses analytical sinusoidal eigenmodes:
\begin{equation}
    \phi_{j, m} = \sin\left(\frac{j m \pi}{N-1}\right), \quad j, m = 1, \dots, N-2,
\end{equation}
the system can be diagonalized exactly using the Type-I Discrete Sine Transform (DST-I)~\cite{swarztrauber1977methods}. Applying the 2D DST-I to both sides of $\nabla_h^2 \psi = -\omega$:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\psi}_{k_x, k_y} = \frac{\hat{\omega}_{k_x, k_y}}{\lambda_{k_x} + \lambda_{k_y}}, \quad \lambda_k = \frac{4}{h^2} \sin^2\left(\frac{k \pi}{2(N-1)}\right).
\end{equation}
Computing the 2D inverse DST-I yields $\psi_{i, j}$ exact to machine precision ($\sim 10^{-16}$) in $\mathcal{O}(N^2 \log N)$ operations via fast FFT algorithms (\texttt{scipy.fft.dst}). In benchmark timings on workstation hardware (Intel Core i7-12700H, 16 threads, SciPy FFTW backend), this spectral solve executes in approximately $0.8\,\text{ms}$ on $257 \times 257$ and $2.1\,\text{ms}$ on $513 \times 513$, completely removing Poisson convergence tolerance as an iterative bottleneck.

\subsection{Vectorized Alternating Direction Implicit (ADI) Marching}
The vorticity transport equation (\ref{eq:vorticity_transport}) is advanced in pseudo- or physical-time using an operator-split Alternating Direction Implicit (ADI) scheme based on the Peaceman--Rachford structure. To decouple the spatial coordinates while enabling high-performance vectorized tridiagonal solves across entire grid tensors, the convective flux $\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{u}^n, \omega^n) = u^n \delta_x \omega^n + v^n \delta_y \omega^n$ is evaluated explicitly at time level $n$, while viscous diffusion is split implicitly along alternating coordinate axes across two half-steps of size $\Delta t / 2$:
\begin{align}
    \frac{\omega^* - \omega^n}{\Delta t / 2} - \frac{1}{Re}\delta_x^2 \omega^* &= \frac{1}{Re}\delta_y^2 \omega^n - \left( u^n \delta_x \omega^n + v^n \delta_y \omega^n \right), \label{eq:adi_step1} \\
    \frac{\omega^{n+1} - \omega^*}{\Delta t / 2} - \frac{1}{Re}\delta_y^2 \omega^{n+1} &= \frac{1}{Re}\delta_x^2 \omega^* - \left( u^n \delta_x \omega^n + v^n \delta_y \omega^n \right), \label{eq:adi_step2}
\end{align}
where $\delta_x^2$ and $\delta_y^2$ denote standard second-order central difference Laplacian operators. Because the convective flux $\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{u}^n, \omega^n)$ is evaluated explicitly at time level $n$ while viscous diffusion is split implicitly across the two half-steps, the resulting operator-split ADI scheme possesses a formal temporal splitting accuracy of $\mathcal{O}(\Delta t) + \mathcal{O}(h^2)$ for frozen advective fields, with unconditional linear stability for the diffusive operator. Because viscous diffusion alone is inverted implicitly in each coordinate direction, the resulting tridiagonal linear systems possess spatially invariant scalar coefficients along each grid line:
\begin{equation}
    A = -\frac{\alpha_{\text{ADI}}}{2}, \quad B = 1 + \alpha_{\text{ADI}}, \quad C = -\frac{\alpha_{\text{ADI}}}{2}, \quad \text{with} \quad \alpha_{\text{ADI}} = \frac{\Delta t}{Re \cdot h^2}.
\end{equation}
This allows precomputation of the Thomas factorization arrays $(P, \text{denom})$ and admits complete vectorized forward and backward sweeps across all $M = N-2$ rows (in Step 1) and columns (in Step 2) simultaneously via NumPy vectorization, bypassing per-node iterative loops.

Crucially, for all $Re \le 50,000$, all convective derivatives are discretized using \emph{pure second-order central differences}:
\begin{equation}
    (u \delta_x \omega)_{i, j} = u_{i, j} \frac{\omega_{i+1, j} - \omega_{i-1, j}}{2 h}, \quad (v \delta_y \omega)_{i, j} = v_{i, j} \frac{\omega_{i, j+1} - \omega_{i, j-1}}{2 h}.
\end{equation}
In this regime, the leading truncation error of the spatial convective stencil introduces zero artificial numerical viscosity ($\nu_{\text{num}} = 0$ in the spatial modified equation).

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Cell P\'eclet Number Transition, Boundary Layer Scaling, and Interior Hybrid Stabilization ($Re \ge 100,000$)}{Cell Peclet Number Transition, Boundary Layer Scaling, and Interior Hybrid Stabilization (Re >= 100000)}}
In finite-difference formulations of convection-diffusion systems, the local cell P\'eclet number governs the monotonicity and spectral stability of central differences. We define the signed cell P\'eclet components:
\begin{equation}
    \widetilde{Pe}_{x, i, j} = \frac{u_{i, j} h}{\nu} = Re \cdot u_{i, j} \cdot h, \quad \widetilde{Pe}_{y, i, j} = \frac{v_{i, j} h}{\nu} = Re \cdot v_{i, j} \cdot h,
\end{equation}
with magnitudes $Pe_{x, i, j} = |\widetilde{Pe}_{x, i, j}|$, $Pe_{y, i, j} = |\widetilde{Pe}_{y, i, j}|$, and maximum local directional magnitude $Pe_{h, i, j} = \max(Pe_x, Pe_y)$.

The classical linear stability limit requires $Pe_h \le 2$ to prevent spurious, high-wavenumber spatial dispersion (odd-even node decoupling). On a uniform $513 \times 513$ mesh ($h = 1/512 \approx 1.953 \times 10^{-3}$) with lid velocity $U = 1.0$, the maximum cell P\'eclet number scales linearly with Reynolds number:
\begin{align}
    Re = 1,000  &\implies Pe \approx 1.95 < 2 \quad \text{(unconditionally monotonic)}, \notag \\
    Re = 10,000 &\implies Pe \approx 19.53, \notag \\
    Re = 50,000 &\implies Pe \approx 97.66, \notag \\
    Re = 100,000 &\implies Pe \approx 195.31 \gg 2.
\end{align}
Simultaneously, the physical laminar boundary layer thickness scales asymptotically as $\delta \sim L / \sqrt{Re}$. At $Re = 100,000$, the nominal boundary layer thickness contracts to $\delta \approx 1/\sqrt{100,000} \approx 3.16 \times 10^{-3}$, meaning that a $513 \times 513$ grid possesses only $\delta / h \approx 1.62$ grid intervals across the wall shear layer (omitting an $\mathcal{O}(1)$ laminar constant).

At the discontinuous driving lid corners $(x, y) = (0, 1)$ and $(1, 1)$, where the velocity abruptly jumps from $u_{\text{lid}} = 1$ to $u_{\text{wall}} = 0$, the mathematical singularity produces unbounded local vorticity gradients $\partial \omega / \partial x \to \infty$. When $Pe_h \approx 195.3 \gg 2$, pure central differencing excites unphysical high-frequency dispersive waves that propagate inward along the sidewalls, eventually inducing nonlinear numerical divergence.

To overcome this singularity shock while maintaining stability on uniform grids, we incorporate an interior hybrid differencing formulation based on local directional cell P\'eclet numbers for extreme Reynolds numbers ($Re \ge 100{,}000$). Using the signed cell P\'eclet numbers, the convective derivatives are evaluated piecewise with reachable directional branches:
\begin{equation}
    (u \delta_x \omega)_{i, j} = \begin{cases}
        u_{i, j} \frac{\omega_{i+1, j} - \omega_{i-1, j}}{2 h}, & |\widetilde{Pe}_{x, i, j}| \le 2, \\
        u_{i, j} \frac{\omega_{i, j} - \omega_{i-1, j}}{h}, & \widetilde{Pe}_{x, i, j} > 2, \\
        u_{i, j} \frac{\omega_{i+1, j} - \omega_{i, j}}{h}, & \widetilde{Pe}_{x, i, j} < -2,
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
with an identical directional formulation along the $y$-axis based on $\widetilde{Pe}_{y, i, j}$.

In our operator-split ADI implementation, the implicit tridiagonal operators retain the full physical diffusion $(1/Re)\nabla^2 \omega$ in both half-steps; molecular diffusion is not dropped when upwinding activates. From modified equation analysis, first-order directional upwinding of $u \partial \omega / \partial x$ introduces a leading truncation error of $\frac{|u| h}{2} \frac{\partial^2 \omega}{\partial x^2}$. Because the implicit ADI half-steps invert the complete molecular diffusion operator $\nu \nabla^2 \omega$, the effective total diffusion in an activated coordinate direction is $\nu_{\text{eff}, x} = \nu + \nu_{\text{art}, x}$, where the added artificial numerical diffusion is:
\begin{equation}
    \nu_{\text{art}, x} = \frac{|u_{i, j}| h}{2} \mathbf{1}_{Pe_x > 2}, \quad \nu_{\text{art}, y} = \frac{|v_{i, j}| h}{2} \mathbf{1}_{Pe_y > 2}.
\end{equation}
The non-dimensional artificial-to-molecular viscosity ratio is therefore $\nu_{\text{art}, x}/\nu = \frac{1}{2} Pe_x \mathbf{1}_{Pe_x > 2}$. Defining the isotropic scalar added numerical viscosity metric as the directional average:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\bar{\nu}_{\text{art}}}{\nu} = \frac{1}{2}\left( \frac{\nu_{\text{art}, x}}{\nu} + \frac{\nu_{\text{art}, y}}{\nu} \right) = \frac{1}{4} \left[ Pe_x \mathbf{1}_{Pe_x > 2} + Pe_y \mathbf{1}_{Pe_y > 2} \right]. \label{eq:excess_visc}
\end{equation}
Evaluating Eq.~(\ref{eq:excess_visc}) at $Re = 100{,}000$ on the $513 \times 513$ mesh: at the cavity geometric center $(0.5, 0.5)$, velocities are small ($u \approx -0.0304, v \approx -0.0036$), yielding directional P\'eclet numbers $Pe_x \approx 5.94$ and $Pe_y \approx 0.70$ ($Pe_y \le 2$). Because only the $x$-direction activates upwinding, the added numerical viscosity ratio is $\bar{\nu}_{\text{art}} / \nu = \frac{1}{4}(5.94) = 1.485 \approx 1.49$. Along the driving lid ($|u| = 1.0, v = 0$), $Pe_x = 195.31$, yielding an added ratio of $\bar{\nu}_{\text{art}} / \nu = \frac{1}{4}(195.31) = 48.83$ (with maximum discrete grid value $48.93$).

Figure~\ref{fig:peclet_dissipation_map} provides a spatial audit of the hybrid differencing regime and numerical dissipation at $Re = 100,000$. Pure central differencing ($Pe \le 2, \bar{\nu}_{\text{art}} = 0$) is active across $1.79\%$ of the domain area, concentrated along lines of zero velocity and near stagnation points. Across the remaining $98.21\%$ of the interior domain, the hybrid formulation activates directional upwinding, providing domain-wide interior numerical stabilization that suppresses high-wavenumber dispersive corner shock waves. 

Importantly, as depicted in Figure~\ref{fig:peclet_dissipation_map}(c), the magnitude of numerical dissipation is strongly non-uniform. In the cavity geometric core $(0.5, 0.5)$, the added viscosity ratio is $\bar{\nu}_{\text{art}} / \nu = 1.485$ ($\bar{\nu}_{\text{art}} \approx 1.49\nu$). The peak numerical viscosity ratio $\bar{\nu}_{\text{art}} / \nu = 48.83$ (discrete maximum $48.93$) is confined strictly to the high-speed near-lid shear layer ($Pe_{\max} = 195.31$). 

Upon grid refinement to $1025 \times 1025$ ($h = 1/1024$), the cell spacing is halved, which directly suppresses numerical dissipation: the pure central differencing area expands to $3.09\%$, the peak P\'eclet number drops by half to $Pe_{\max} = 97.66$ ($\bar{\nu}_{\text{art}} / \nu \le 24.46$), the center added dissipation drops to $\bar{\nu}_{\text{art}} / \nu = 0.74$ (less than three-quarters of physical molecular viscosity), and the mean added viscosity over the cavity drops from $16.32\nu$ to $8.12\nu$.

We emphasize that for all moderate and high Reynolds numbers up to $Re = 50,000$, our solver executes with \emph{pure second-order central differencing everywhere} ($\nu_{\text{art}} = 0$ identically across $100\%$ of the domain). The hybrid formulation is invoked selectively at $Re = 100,000$ to provide a stable continuation benchmark and suppress corner singularity dispersion.

\subsection{Reynolds Number Homotopy Continuation Ladder}
At extreme Reynolds numbers ($Re \ge 5,000$), impulsive starts from quiescent initial conditions ($\mathbf{u} = 0, \psi = 0$) generate catastrophic corner boundary layer detachments that destabilize central difference operators. To overcome this startup shock, we implement a parameter homotopy continuation ladder:
\begin{align}
    \mathcal{H}(Re) : 100 &\longrightarrow 1,000 \longrightarrow 3,200 \longrightarrow 5,000 \longrightarrow 7,500 \longrightarrow 10,000 \notag \\
    &\longrightarrow 15,000 \longrightarrow 20,000 \longrightarrow 25,000 \longrightarrow 30,000 \longrightarrow 35,000 \notag \\
    &\longrightarrow 40,000 \longrightarrow 45,000 \longrightarrow 50,000 \longrightarrow 100,000.
\end{align}
The converged velocity and vorticity fields of stage $Re_m$ serve as the warm-start initial condition for stage $Re_{m+1}$. For inter-grid transitions, a smooth bicubic spline interpolation operator prolongs coarse-mesh flow fields to ultra-fine meshes with boundary re-normalization.

\section{Stationary Continuation Benchmark Results (\texorpdfstring{$Re = 100 \to 100,000$}{Re = 100 to 100000})}

For Reynolds numbers above the primary instability threshold ($Re > Re_{\text{cr}} \approx 8,000$), steady states of the physical Navier--Stokes system lose temporal stability to oscillatory perturbations. In this regime, the solutions obtained via pseudo-transient continuation represent stationary continuation branches (unstable fixed points of the continuous dynamical system), rather than stable physical attractors. \subsection{\texorpdfstring{Core Vorticity Homogenization \& Batchelor's Hypothesis ($Re \to 100,000$)}{Core Vorticity Homogenization and Batchelor's Hypothesis (Re -> 100,000)}}
A prominent physical feature of two-dimensional recirculating flows at high Reynolds numbers is the homogenization of vorticity within closed streamline regions. According to the classical asymptotic theorem of Batchelor (1956)~\cite{batchelor1956steady}, in the limit $Re \to \infty$, steady Navier--Stokes solutions with closed streamlines must approach a state of uniform core vorticity $\nabla \omega \approx 0$:
\begin{equation}
    \lim_{Re \to \infty} \nabla \omega(x, y) = 0 \quad \text{for } (x, y) \in \Omega_{\text{core}}.
\end{equation}
Batchelor's theorem establishes spatial uniformity of the core vorticity rather than prescribing a specific numerical value $\omega_0$. For an idealized square cavity with regularized boundary layers and uniform perimeter dissipation, Burggraf (1966)~\cite{burggraf1966analytical} derived an asymptotic theoretical limit of $\omega_0 \approx -1.886$ (under the present clockwise sign convention). In finite-difference simulations at finite Reynolds numbers ($Re = 10,000$--$100,000$), our computed vorticity across the central core span ($y \in [0.35, 0.65]$ along $x = 0.5$) exhibits a flat plateau with a standard deviation of approximately $0.10$, settling at $\omega_0 = -2.5418$ at $Re = 100,000$ (and $-2.5719$ at $Re = 10,000$, $-2.5453$ at $Re = 50,000$). These profiles are qualitatively consistent with Batchelor's core homogenization hypothesis. The offset from Burggraf's $-1.886$ inviscid limit is a consequence of the singular top-corner velocity discontinuities ($u = 1 \to 0$), the displacement effect of large corner recirculation eddies (which constrict the primary core perimeter), and interior hybrid upwind stabilization at extreme $Re$.

Concurrently, high-Reynolds numerical continuation solutions exhibit an accompanying geometric restructuring: as viscous wall boundary layers contract and secondary eddy systems reach topological balance, the primary recirculating core center migrates horizontally from $(0.5117, 0.5215)$ at $Re = 10,000$ toward the geometric mid-plane ($x_c \to 0.50000$ at $Re = 50,000$, and $0.4990$ on $1025 \times 1025$), while the core minimum streamfunction stabilizes at $\psi_{\min} \approx -0.12572$.

\section{Unsteady Dynamics, Vortex Shedding \& Limit-Cycle Analysis}

\subsection{Unsteady Vortex Shedding on \texorpdfstring{$513 \times 513$}{513x513} (\texorpdfstring{$Re = 10,000$}{Re = 10000})}
In physical fluid flow, the steady state of the lid-driven cavity undergoes a supercritical Hopf bifurcation. While spectral collocation investigations using smooth, regularized lid velocities (e.g., Peng, Shiau \& Hwang 2003~\cite{peng2003transition}) identify the linear instability onset near $Re_{\text{cr}} \approx 7,400$ ($St \approx 0.59$), finite-difference formulations with discontinuous lid corners (e.g., Bruneau \& Saad 2006~\cite{bruneau20062d}) identify the primary instability threshold in the interval $8,000 \le Re_{\text{cr}} \le 8,050$ with shedding frequency near $St \approx 0.45$ at $Re = 8,100$.

To investigate post-bifurcation limit-cycle dynamics and establish temporal accuracy, we performed physical time-accurate marching without wall relaxation ($\beta_{\text{wall}} = 1.0$) directly on the $513 \times 513$ ultra-fine mesh over $44,000$ time steps ($\Delta t = 5.0 \times 10^{-4}$ convective time units, reaching $t = 22.0$ with $CFL \approx 0.256$). A localized spatial perturbation $\delta \omega = 0.05 \exp(-[(x-0.8)^2 + (y-0.8)^2]/0.05^2)$ was seeded into the upper shear layer to trigger the unstable linear mode. \section{Open Benchmark Datasets \& Scientific Reproducibility}
To empower future research in computational fluid dynamics, turbulence modeling, and algorithm verification, all converged solutions are serialized directly into open NumPy archive formats (\texttt{.npz}) containing full 2D coordinate matrices $(X, Y)$, velocities $(u, v)$, streamfunction $(\psi)$, vorticity $(\omega)$, and reconstructed Poisson pressure fields $(p)$. The pressure field $p(x, y)$ is reconstructed in post-processing by solving the diagnostic Poisson equation $\nabla^2 p = 2 [ (\partial u/\partial x)(\partial v/\partial y) - (\partial u/\partial y)(\partial v/\partial x) ]$ with homogeneous Neumann conditions $\partial p / \partial n = 0$ on all walls as a diagnostic surrogate. Users requiring primitive-variable momentum-consistent wall pressure distributions should note this diagnostic boundary treatment.

The complete package includes:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Twenty-Eight (28) Verified Datasets:} Full steady flow fields spanning fifteen Reynolds numbers from $Re = 100$ to $Re = 100,000$, with comprehensive $513 \times 513$ ultra-fine grids for $Re \in \{10000, 15000, 20000, 25000, 30000, 35000, 40000, 45000, 50000, 100000\}$, a $1025 \times 1025$ high-resolution benchmark for $Re = 100,000$, and time-resolved unsteady telemetry for $Re \in \{10000, 15000, 25000, 50000, 100000\}$ strictly on $513 \times 513$ meshes.
    \item \textbf{High-Resolution $513 \times 513$ Animated Visualizations:} Synchronized limit-cycle and multi-frequency vortex shedding animations with embedded real-time probe telemetry across all unsteady regimes up to $Re = 100,000$.
    \item \textbf{Automated CLI Tooling:} Single-command utilities for steady continuation (\path{lid_driven_cavity_fdm.py}), unsteady bifurcation analysis (\path{unsteady_cavity_solver.py}), publication figure reproduction (\path{plot_from_npz.py}), and animated GIF generation.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
This paper presented a high-resolution finite-difference framework for the 2D lid-driven cavity problem, solving stationary continuation up to extreme Reynolds numbers ($Re = 100,000$) on meshes up to $1025 \times 1025$ ($1,050,625$ nodes) and capturing time-accurate unsteady dynamics on $513 \times 513$ grids ($263,169$ nodes). By combining an exact $\mathcal{O}(N^2 \log N)$ Discrete Sine Transform (DST-I) Poisson solver with pure second-order central differencing and homotopy continuation, the solver operates without artificial numerical viscosity across moderate-to-high Reynolds numbers ($Re \le 50,000$). At $Re = 100,000$, an interior hybrid upwind stabilization regularizes high-frequency corner singularity shocks ($Pe_x = 195.3$) with added numerical viscosity ratio $\bar{\nu}_{\text{art}}/\nu = \frac{1}{4}[Pe_x \mathbf{1}_{Pe_x > 2} + Pe_y \mathbf{1}_{Pe_y > 2}]$, yielding $1.485$ in the recirculating core and $48.83$ at the lid on $513 \times 513$ (dropping to $0.74$ and $24.46$ on $1025 \times 1025$). Systematic geometric comparisons against Ghia et al. (1982) and Erturk et al. (2005, 2009) demonstrate primary and secondary vortex center agreement ($\Delta = 0.00688$ at $Re = 25,000$), alongside a consistent $\sim 7.5\%$ streamfunction extremum shift attributable to fine-grid secondary eddy resolution. At $Re = 50,000$ and $Re = 100,000$, the primary core center converges toward mid-plane symmetry ($x_c = 0.50000$ on $513 \times 513$ and $x_c = 0.4990$ on $1025 \times 1025$) with $\psi_{\min} = -0.12572$, showing behavior qualitatively consistent with Batchelor's core vorticity homogenization hypothesis ($\omega_0 \approx -2.54$). Unsteady physical simulations on $513 \times 513$ meshes resolve periodic limit-cycle dynamics ($St = 0.6249 \pm 0.0416$ at $Re = 10,000$, $T \approx 1.6002$ c.t.u.), capture amplitude-modulated multi-frequency dynamics at $Re = 15,000$ ($St = 0.5364 \pm 0.0278$), and trace nonlinear vortex shedding across extended horizons: $Re = 25,000$ ($St = 0.0953 \pm 0.0281$, $T = 10.49$ c.t.u.), $Re = 50,000$ ($St = 0.2581 \pm 0.0300$, $T = 3.88$ c.t.u.), and extreme $Re = 100,000$ ($St = 0.1908 \pm 0.0379$, $T = 5.24$ c.t.u.).

By releasing all source codes, benchmark datasets, and animation pipelines openly and permanently under the MIT license, this work provides a reliable, reproducible, and accessible CFD reference for researchers, students, and practitioners worldwide.
