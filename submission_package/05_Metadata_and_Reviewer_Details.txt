Full Title:
A High-Resolution Finite-Difference Benchmark for Steady Continuation and Hopf Bifurcation Dynamics up to Extreme Reynolds Numbers (Re = 100,000) in 2D Lid-Driven Cavity Flows

Short Running Title:
Extreme-Re Cavity Flow Benchmark & Hopf Dynamics

================================================================================
2. ABSTRACT (247 words - Strictly <= 250 words limit)
===============================================================================
The 2D incompressible lid-driven cavity problem is a canonical CFD benchmark. While classical studies focus on Re <= 10,000, higher-Re regimes lack open tensor datasets and unified bifurcation records. We present a high-resolution finite-difference framework across steady continuation and unsteady regimes from Re = 100 to Re = 100,000. The numerical engine couples an O(N^2 log N) Discrete Sine Transform (DST-I) Poisson solver, eliminating iterative residuals, with vectorized Alternating Direction Implicit marching and parameter homotopy. For Re <= 50,000, pure second-order central differencing is strictly preserved (nu_art = 0). At Re = 100,000 (peak cell Peclet Pe_x ~ 195.3), an interior hybrid upwind stabilization regularizes corner singularity dispersion with added numerical viscosity ratio nu_art_bar / nu = 1/4[Pe_x 1_{Pe_x>2} + Pe_y 1_{Pe_y>2}], yielding 1.485 at the core and 48.83 at the lid on 513 x 513 (dropping to 0.74 and 24.46 on 1025 x 1025). On meshes up to 1025 x 1025, stationary continuation resolves primary and secondary corner eddies, demonstrating geometric agreement with benchmarks while documenting a consistent ~7.5% offset in psi_min. At Re = 50,000 and 100,000, core vorticity exhibits behavior qualitatively consistent with Batchelor's core homogenization hypothesis (omega_0 ~ -2.54) with mid-plane centering (x_c -> 0.50000). Physical marching on 513 x 513 grids (t = 16.0-22.0 c.t.u., St = fL/U) resolves periodic limit cycles at Re = 10,000 (St = 0.6249 +/- 0.0416), captures modulated multi-frequency dynamics at Re = 15,000 (St = 0.5364 +/- 0.0278), and characterizes nonlinear shedding at Re = 25,000 (St = 0.0953 +/- 0.0281), Re = 50,000 (St = 0.2581 +/- 0.0300), and Re = 100,000 (St = 0.1908 +/- 0.0379). Datasets, tools, and animations are openly archived.
