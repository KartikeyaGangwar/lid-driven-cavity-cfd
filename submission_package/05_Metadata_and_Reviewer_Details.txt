2. ABSTRACT (243 words - Strictly <= 250 words limit)
===============================================================================
The two-dimensional incompressible lid-driven cavity problem is a canonical benchmark in computational fluid dynamics. While classical benchmarks focus on Re <= 10,000, higher-Re regimes lack open tensor datasets and unified dynamical descriptions. We present a high-resolution finite-difference framework across steady continuation and finite-window unsteady regimes from Re = 100 to Re = 100,000. The numerical architecture couples an O(N^2 log N) Discrete Sine Transform (DST-I) Poisson solver, eliminating iterative residuals, with vectorized Alternating Direction Implicit marching and parameter continuation. For Re <= 50,000, pure second-order central differencing is preserved (nu_art = 0). At Re = 100,000 (peak cell Peclet Pe_x ~ 195.3), an interior hybrid upwind differencing formulation regularizes corner singularity shocks, which we characterize as a hybrid-regularized numerical model with directional artificial diffusion tensor nu_art. On uniform Cartesian meshes up to 1025 x 1025, stationary continuation resolves primary and secondary corner vortices, demonstrating geometric agreement with benchmarks alongside an observed ~7.5% offset in psi_min. At Re = 50,000 and 100,000, core vorticity exhibits qualitative behavior consistent with Batchelor's core homogenization hypothesis (omega_0 ~ -2.54) with mid-plane symmetry (x_c -> 0.50000). Time-resolved simulations on 513 x 513 grids (t = 16.0-22.0 c.t.u., St = fL/U) capture periodic vortex shedding at Re = 10,000 (St = 0.6249 +/- 0.0416, 7.50 cycles), an amplitude-modulated multi-frequency response at Re = 15,000 (St = 0.5364 +/- 0.0278, 9.66 cycles), and nonlinear shedding at Re = 25,000 (St = 0.0953 +/- 0.0281, 1.70 cycles), Re = 50,000 (St = 0.2581 +/- 0.0300, 4.30 cycles), and Re = 100,000 (St = 0.1908 +/- 0.0379, 2.52 cycles). All thirty datasets, tools, and animations are openly archived.
