2. ABSTRACT (248 words - Strictly <= 250 words limit)
===============================================================================
The 2D incompressible lid-driven cavity problem serves as a canonical model in fluid dynamics. While classical benchmarks (Re <= 10,000) focus on centerline data, open full-field archives spanning moderate to extreme Reynolds numbers (Re = 100 to 100,000) remain scarce. We present a finite-difference framework solving the streamfunction--vorticity equations via an O(N^2 log N) Discrete Sine Transform (DST-I) Poisson solver and vectorized Alternating Direction Implicit (ADI) pseudo-time continuation. For Re <= 50,000, spatial discretization retains pure second-order central differencing (nu_art = 0). At Re = 100,000, where cell Peclet numbers exceed 195, a directional hybrid differencing model regularizes the flow with added numerical viscosity tensor nu_art active across 98.21% of the domain on 513 x 513 (96.91% on 1025 x 1025), yielding directional mean ratio nu_art/nu = 1.485 at the geometric cavity center (0.5, 0.5), an active interior peak of 48.93 at y = 1-h, and domain mean 16.32 (reducing to 0.74, 24.46, and 8.12 on 1025 x 1025). On uniform grids up to 1025 x 1025, stationary continuation resolves primary and secondary eddy topologies, showing qualitative alignment with Batchelor's core homogenization hypothesis (omega_0 ~ -2.54, x_c -> 0.50000). Time-accurate marching on 513 x 513 grids over finite convective windows (t = 16.0-22.0 c.t.u., St = fL/U) characterizes coherent vortex shedding modes at Re = 10,000 (St = 0.6249 +/- 0.0416), amplitude-modulated dynamics at Re = 15,000 (St = 0.5364 +/- 0.0278), and finite-window spectral responses across Re = 25,000, 50,000, and 100,000, where +/- Delta f / 2 denotes discrete Fourier bin half-width. All thirty datasets are openly archived.
