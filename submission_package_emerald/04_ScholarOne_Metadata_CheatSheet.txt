[Title]
A High-Resolution Finite-Difference Framework for Steady Continuation and Finite-Window Unsteady Dynamics up to Re = 100,000 in 2D Lid-Driven Cavity Flows

[Short Title / Running Head] (if requested)
High-Re FDM Cavity Continuation and Dynamics

[Structured Abstract]
(Emerald requires a structured abstract <= 250 words. Word count below: 223 words)

Purpose:
To resolve the computational bottlenecks of iterative residual drift, artificial numerical dissipation, and high-Reynolds divergence in the canonical 2D incompressible lid-driven cavity problem from Re = 100 up to Re = 100,000.

Design/methodology/approach:
A finite-difference framework solves the streamfunction-vorticity equations by coupling an exact O(N^2 log N) Discrete Sine Transform (DST-I) Poisson solver with vectorized Alternating Direction Implicit (ADI) marching. Pure second-order central differencing (zero artificial viscosity, nu_art = 0) is preserved for Re <= 50,000. At Re = 100,000 (cell Peclet > 195), an interior directional hybrid model regularizes driven corner singularities with explicit numerical dissipation mapping.

Findings:
Stationary continuation on Cartesian meshes up to 1025 x 1025 captures multi-scale corner eddy cascades and demonstrates qualitative alignment with Batchelor's core homogenization hypothesis (vortex center x_c -> 0.50000, plateau core vorticity omega_0 ~ -2.54). Spatial discretization achieves ASME V&V 20 grid convergence (GCI = 0.48%). Time-accurate marching on 513 x 513 meshes resolves coherent vortex shedding at Re = 10,000 (St = 0.6249), modulated dynamics at Re = 15,000 (St = 0.5364), and finite-window shedding modes up to Re = 100,000.

Originality/value:
This study provides an open-source, highly scalable spectral-accelerated FDM framework, extends verified benchmark solutions to extreme Reynolds numbers (Re = 100,000), and openly releases thirty validated full-field datasets.
