\noindent\textbf{Structured Abstract (223 words):}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Purpose:} To resolve the computational bottlenecks of iterative residual drift, artificial numerical dissipation, and high-Reynolds divergence in the canonical 2D incompressible lid-driven cavity problem from $Re = 100$ up to $Re = 100{,}000$.
    \item \textbf{Design/methodology/approach:} A finite-difference framework solves the streamfunction--vorticity equations by coupling an exact $\mathcal{O}(N^2 \log N)$ Discrete Sine Transform (DST-I) Poisson solver with vectorized Alternating Direction Implicit (ADI) marching. Pure second-order central differencing (zero artificial viscosity, $\nu_{\mathrm{art}} = 0$) is preserved for $Re \le 50{,}000$. At $Re = 100{,}000$ (cell P\'eclet $> 195$), an interior directional hybrid model regularizes driven corner singularities with explicit numerical dissipation mapping.
    \item \textbf{Findings:} Stationary continuation on Cartesian meshes up to $1025 \times 1025$ captures multi-scale corner eddy cascades and demonstrates qualitative alignment with Batchelor's core homogenization hypothesis (vortex center $x_c \to 0.50000$, plateau core vorticity $\omega_0 \approx -2.54$). Spatial discretization achieves ASME V\&V 20 grid convergence ($\mathrm{GCI} = 0.48\%$). Time-accurate marching on $513 \times 513$ meshes resolves coherent vortex shedding at $Re = 10{,}000$ ($St = 0.6249$), modulated dynamics at $Re = 15{,}000$ ($St = 0.5364$), and finite-window shedding modes up to $Re = 100{,}000$.
    \item \textbf{Originality/value:} This study provides an open-source, highly scalable spectral-accelerated FDM framework, extends verified benchmark solutions to extreme Reynolds numbers ($Re = 100{,}000$), and openly releases thirty validated full-field datasets.
\end{itemize}
